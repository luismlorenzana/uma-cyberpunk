The board

Status: 2026-11-13, CY7 (D11), the post layer tuned (D17). The battle board as apps/web/src/board/ draws it, on uma-stage 0.5.0's parts, and its test bench at /board. The sandbox (CY8) plays battles on it. The kit is uma-stage's stand-in forest under a night grade until the first world's kit (Phase 4).

The short answer

  • The map's heights become terrain exactly as written: digit rows 0–3 are uma-stage's own format, passed straight to roomGeometry (never through parseRoomDesign, which stops at level 2). Solid tiles are walls.
  • Umas are sprites, enemies and guests are standees, each on a base ring in her side's colour with a facing wedge pointing the way she faces.
  • The overlays paint tile tops in TC29's colours. A tap picks a tile; a drag pans; the camera never turns or zooms.
  • It draws only when something changes, and the battery saver drops it to 30 fps with no post effects.

The overlays

One merged mesh over the tile tops, rebuilt when the set changes:

ColourMeans
BlueShe can reach it, and no enemy can strike it next turn
MagentaShe can reach it, inside an enemy's reach
RedAn enemy she could strike from a tile she can reach
YellowAn area (a mortar, a smoke charge), from CY8
A white outlineThe cursor: the tile last tapped

Taps and the pan

  • A tap picks the tile under the finger: a unit's tile when the tap lands on her (her card is about a thumb wide), else the nearest tile top. A tap on a raised tile's front face picks the tile it belongs to only if its top is under the finger, as the stage's picking does everywhere.
  • A drag pans on both axes, clamped to the board's edges; the camera snaps to the pixel grid, so the art stays crisp.
  • No rotation and no zoom: the camera is uma-stage's, pitched 35°, 128 texels a tile. A tile is about 24 pt deep on a 3× phone held sideways, just under the 44 pt rule, which is why the sandbox moves in two taps (TC29).
  • On an upright phone the stage is turned (uma-stage's TurnedStage), and touches go through toStage.

The fade

High ground (level 2 and up) is built apart from the rest. While a tile of it stands in front of the unit in focus (the one selected, acting or targeted), it eases to 35% opacity over 150 ms, at once with reduced motion; a unit's own tile never fades for her.

The loop and the saver

  • Render on change: a tap, a pan, a walk, a fade or a resize wakes the loop; it sleeps three frames after the last change. A hidden page draws nothing.
  • The battery saver (on by default on phones, initialSaver()): 30 fps through FramePacer, and no tilt-shift or bloom. With it off, uma-stage's Effects draws both with the board's own values (BOARD_EFFECTS, D17), which the first stage request made possible:

The test bench

/board (behind sign-in, loaded on demand with three.js and the stage) puts thirteen stand-ins on a 14 × 14 map with heights 0–3 (?map=flat-14 for the flat one): four Umas as sprites, a guest and eight enemies as standees. Tap a unit for her reach, tap a blue or magenta tile to walk her there, drag to pan. Back returns Home; Saver toggles it.

?debug adds a readout (figures, sprites and live sets, draw calls, fps, the saver, the device size, faded tiles) and window.__board: step(ms), pick(x, y), figures, drawCalls, fps, saver, debug, setSaver(on), tap(x, z) and record(ms), which pans for ms while recording frames. The preview pane draws WebGL but runs no animation frames while hidden, so checks call step() and read these.
