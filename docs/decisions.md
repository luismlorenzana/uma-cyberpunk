# Decisions Log — uma-cyberpunk

Game decisions for uma-cyberpunk. Cross-game decisions (`P#`) are in uma-platform `docs/decisions.md`; this repo starts
with P144. Newest entries at the bottom. To reverse a decision, add a new entry that supersedes it instead of editing
history. Fetch `main` before taking a number.

**The concept's calls keep their ids** (D1): TC1–TC16 in [the concept](design/concept.md).

| # | Date | Decision | Why / Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | 2026-11-12 | **The repo starts, and adopts the concept.** The owner asked for a new repo for "uma-cyberpunk", a tactics RPG with a science-fiction and space story, crossing Uma Musume with other mobile RPG franchises, with a simple doc to build on. `luismlorenzana/uma-cyberpunk` is created **private** (uma-platform P1), with uma-quest's settings: issues on, wiki off, every merge style allowed, a read-only workflow token. It goes public only on the owner's word. The first commit copies uma-quest's: the private-content guard in CI, `.gitignore` with `/content/` ignored, `.npmrc` for GitHub Packages, Node 22. The concept's sixteen calls (TC1–TC16) are adopted as recommendations; the owner's review of the concept page can change any of them with a new row here. **Ids:** decisions D1 on, packets CY1 on, concept calls TC (CY and TC appear in no other Uma repo; C and CP do) | The owner asked for the repo first and a doc to build on, so a session here starts from this repo alone. Copying uma-quest's first commit keeps every Uma repo set up the same way. A private repo spends Actions minutes from the free 2,000 a month, which the guard alone barely touches |
| D2 | 2026-11-12 | **Phase 1 is three packets: CY1, CY2 and CY3, as in uma-quest and uma-farm.** **CY1**, the workspace: pnpm and turbo; `@cyber/web`, `@cyber/api` and `@cyber/domain` as placeholders installing the six platform packages (uma-stage too, TC10); lint, test and build, the size check at 180 KB, the guard; `.claude/launch.json` on **8121** (API) and **5214** (web), the next pair after uma-platform's 8111 and 5204. **CY2**, the cloud in the `uma-rpg` project, each piece its own (TC11): site `uma-cyberpunk`, Cloud Run `cyber-api`, Firestore database `cyber`, its runtime account, deployer and image repository; written when CY1 is done, run on the owner's go with a free-tier cost table. **CY3**, the hello app: uma-rpg's stack, Google sign-in, save v1 synced through `uma-server`, the update prompt and the Home Screen. CY1 is written now; CY2 and CY3 when CY1 is done | The cloud plan's order (workspace, then cloud, then the app) worked for both new apps. Writing CY1 now keeps the queue ahead of the work; CY2 needs the owner's go anyway, so its packet waits for the cost table it has to carry |
| D3 | 2026-11-12 | **The owner's direction reshapes the concept: Front Mission's rigs on Triangle Strategy's boards, a world from 13 Sentinels and Star Guardian, and the eight franchises as enemy factions.** The owner, taking ownership to Claude: a spinoff using "Uma Musume, the Characters only", like uma-quest and uma-farm; lore and story from *13 Sentinels: Aegis Rim* and League of Legends' *Star Guardian*; gameplay mainly from *Front Mission*, "instead of Robots fight it would be the characters fighting with accessories"; the HD-2D look and the in-game build-out from *Triangle Strategy*; "the other franchises we have added as enemy factions and overall enemies … tied to the plot". Seven research passes ran in parallel (the four references, the eight franchises, uma-stage's grid readiness, the sibling games' conventions). (1) **[references.md](design/references.md)** records what each reference does and what the game takes, with sources; it names no character. (2) **[world.md](design/world.md), WD1–WD24,** is the world bible: the Ring runs on light siphoned from other worlds through its Gates (the Tithe), the siphons let in the Blackout, the squad's rigs run on stolen siphon cores, and the Umas came through the first Gate themselves; five layers revealed over five acts; heroines held by an Anchor, a Collar or nothing; eight factions with original names, one mechanic each. It's original and names no character, so it's in git; casting, chapters and dialogue stay private. (3) **[The concept](design/concept.md) amends TC2–TC10, TC12, TC13, TC15 and TC16 and adds TC17–TC48:** the rig (Core, two arms and Legs break; Visor and Back unit are slots), Capacity, four weapon bands with P, I and F against a Coat, Charge (one pip bar for skills, counters and support), automatic counters, Crossfire and Cover, skills by part mastery, facing and height numbers, five terrain states, Rig Break instead of death, Jack-Out and freeing instead of killing, Strain, the side-view cut-in, the forecast card; acts of Operations and Routes with flash-forwards, Leads, the Archive and the Call; the Hideout's six stations; the cast as the Ranker's 100 by outfit, two batches an act; roles from an authored tag with running style as Stride; NIKKE added as the eighth franchise; stand-in art from master data's chibi sets and renders and the Ranker's images. (4) **CY4's spike** now proves a game-owned battle scene from uma-stage's parts, and files only the stage requests it shows are needed (TC48, seven listed). Phases 1 and CY1 are unchanged | The owner set the five directions; the research found where they meet: the Drain and the Tithe are one idea, Triangle Strategy's TP and Front Mission's AP fold into one resource, the platform's side-on combat art is Front Mission's cut-in, and no reference kills its heroes. Roles follow the platform's move away from running style (P123, uma-quest SC7, uma-farm RM6), and the cast and keys follow P131 and P139. Using art that already exists (chibi sets, renders, the Ranker's images) means no new character art blocks Phases 2 and 3 |
| D4 | 2026-11-12 | **The story moves to its own session, the world becomes interplanetary with Octopath Traveler's structure, and the game's work starts with the app and the combat testing.** The owner, after D3: "for Story authoring, I'm working with a separate session, so maybe we split the work where you just do the brief for Story authoring here which can have some world-building impact … and then the actual work packets for us would be starting with the full app buildout and some of the combat testing side of things … see what we can reuse from the uma-platform HD-2D engine"; and for the world, "an interplanetary type world, where maybe the whole system you described is only Chapter 1 for every Uma and then after finishing that they join into 'the spaceship' which would act as the hub or stronghold, then, when traveling to other worlds there would be other Umas to pickup on their own Chapter 1, a little like the Octopath Traveler approach … but again, the story and world-building would be authored by another session". (1) **The split:** [the story brief](design/story-brief.md) (SB1–SB11, with a page) hands the world and the story to the story session: who owns what, what's fixed (Umas only; heroines freed by their Hold; one mechanic a faction, two new at most a world; franchise worlds off the map; privacy), the hooks the game builds, Octopath's lessons, the first pass carried across worlds, twelve questions with defaults, the content contract sketch (`content/stories/`, outfit ids, board ids, our slugs, uma-quest's chapter format with slots and `aboard`), the deliveries in Uma Story's layers, and a one-line start. [world.md](design/world.md) becomes its first pass, no longer adopted calls. (2) **The concept, amended:** sixteen calls (TC6, TC8–TC10, TC12, TC13, TC15, TC31–TC34, TC39, TC40, TC42, TC45, TC48), five reworded for the ship, and **TC49–TC54 new**: the ship grows a room per lead; the Bridge's star map and each world's node map; lead Umas Octopath's way (Chapter 1 on her world brings her aboard at the squad's level, interjection slots and debriefs, crossed paths twice per lead, a signposted finale); six talents; the story session and its content contract; combat testing three ways. The Hideout becomes the ship's seven rooms. (3) **Research:** an Octopath pass (both games and their reception; added to the references as §7) and two audits: `uma-stage` is **0.3.0**, a kit of parts whose two finished scenes (uma-quest's field, uma-farm's band) don't fit a grid, so the board is a game-owned scene from its parts; its bloom threshold (0.86 on luminance) means saturated neon never blooms, frames can't glow, a biome kit must carry dungeon frames and loads one page per kind, and the saver turns every effect off; heights 0–3 may already build from a design made directly. No sibling has grid battle code. uma-platform **P147** (same day) moved all non-character art to Claude in code, map kits included; CLAUDE.md and TC12 follow. (4) **[The build plan](design/build-plan.md)** (BP1–BP9, with a page): a pure `@cyber/battle` beside `@cyber/domain`; the reuse map (import the six packages, copy uma-quest's wiring, save sync, PWA and runner, uma-farm's story delivery, uma-rpg's place registry and star chart, follow uma-rpg's reducer, autosave-by-replay and AI, uma-racer's simulator); the board's five checks and the likely stage requests; the simulator's targets; the app on test content. (5) **The packets CY2–CY12 are written ahead,** in three phases; Phase 2 starts beside the cloud; CY1 now pins uma-stage 0.3.0. The handoff gains a Story requests table. | The owner split the story out and widened the world; building the app and the combat on test content first means the story drops into a finished game, and the audits show what's free to reuse and what the stage must still learn |
