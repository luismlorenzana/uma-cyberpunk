# Uma Cyberpunk: the references

> **Status: 2026-11-12, the owner's direction (D3).** The owner took the concept further with five directions:
> 1. **A spinoff that uses Uma Musume's characters only,** in the same vein as uma-quest and uma-farm.
> 2. **Lore and story** from *13 Sentinels: Aegis Rim* and League of Legends' *Star Guardian* series.
> 3. **Gameplay** mainly from the *Front Mission* games, with the characters fighting in combat accessories instead of
>    robots.
> 4. **The HD-2D look and the in-game build-out** from *Triangle Strategy*.
> 5. **The other franchises already on the platform** as the enemy factions and the overall enemies, tied to the plot.
>
> Seven research passes ran in parallel on 2026-11-12: one per reference, one on the eight franchises, one on what
> `uma-stage` can do for a grid, and one on how uma-quest and uma-farm use the Umas. This page is what they found and
> what the game takes from each. The calls themselves live in [the concept](concept.md) (TC1–TC48) and
> [the world](world.md) (WD1–WD24). Visual page: [previews/references.html](previews/references.html).
>
> **We borrow structure and ideas only.** No reference's names, terms, characters, plot, art or music goes into the
> game, and this page names no character from any of them.

## The short answer

| Reference | What it's known for | What the game takes | Calls |
|---|---|---|---|
| **13 Sentinels: Aegis Rim** | A puzzle-box story told through many protagonists' routes, played in any order, that lock and unlock each other; an archive where the player assembles the truth | Acts open on a flash-forward; each act has an **Operations** track and Uma-led **Routes** that gate each other; **Leads** and an **Archive** of Files and Anomalies; **Strain** rotates the bench; every reveal is seeded three times | TC27, TC31–TC35, WD12, WD15–WD18 |
| **Star Guardian** | Magical-girl guardians powered by a cosmic source, secret double lives, a corrupting darkness, fallen guardians who return as enemies, redemption by purification | The squad's **double life** and **suit-up**; one hue per Uma; a sincere **rival squad**; heroines arrive **corrupted, controlled or deceived**, and each can be won back | TC26, TC39–TC41, WD1–WD9, WD16 |
| **Front Mission** | Wanzers built from parts (body, two arms, legs), each with its own HP; weight against output; part breaks that change the fight; links; the side-view battle cutaway | The **combat rig**: four breakable parts and two slots; a **Capacity** budget; its three range bands (plus Tech for hacks) and three damage types; automatic **counters and links**; skills learned from parts; jobs as **roles**; the side-view **cut-in**; **Rig Break**, never death; **Jack-Out** to free a heroine; buy, salvage and remodel | TC5, TC17–TC28, TC43 |
| **Triangle Strategy** | HD-2D on block-built maps with height; turn order by speed; TP; follow-up attacks; elemental terrain; a voting system over choices; the Encampment between battles | The **look** on uma-stage at a fixed angle; **Charge** (its TP); **Crossfire** (its follow-ups); **terrain states**; the **forecast card** and range colours; **the Call** (its vote); the **Hideout** as an Encampment with a **Sim Pod** | TC3, TC10, TC20, TC21, TC23, TC24, TC29, TC30, TC35, TC36, TC42, TC44 |
| **The eight franchises** | Azur Lane, Genshin Impact, Wuthering Waves, Honkai: Star Rail, Zenless Zone Zero, Honkai Impact 3rd, Punishing: Gray Raven, NIKKE | **Eight enemy factions**, one per franchise, each with an original name, a reason to be here, a way to turn and a battle identity of its own | TC37, TC38, WD10–WD14 |

**Where the references agree.** The strongest findings are the ones two passes reached on their own:
- **The central truth.** The 13 Sentinels pass proposed "the Drain" (the Ring's power comes from Gates that bleed other
  worlds) and the Star Guardian pass proposed "the Tithe" (the Ring's Authority siphons other worlds' suns). They're the
  same idea, from a mystery angle and a cosmology angle, so [the world](world.md) is built on it (WD1).
- **One resource for the turn.** Triangle Strategy's TP and Front Mission's spend-or-keep AP collapse into one small bar,
  **Charge** (TC20): skills spend it, counters and links need it, waiting banks it.
- **The attack cut-in.** Front Mission cuts to a side view on every attack, and the platform's adult combat art is
  drawn side-on, facing one way. So the cut-in is where the adult art lives, and the map uses the four-facing chibi
  sets (TC12, TC28).
- **No death.** Front Mission's pilot walks away from a wrecked body; Star Guardian's guardians fall and are redeemed;
  uma-quest never maims an Uma. Defeat is a **Rig Break** for the squad and a **freeing** for a heroine (TC25, TC26).

## 1. 13 Sentinels: Aegis Rim (story structure)

Vanillaware and Atlus; Japan 2019, worldwide 2020, Switch 2022. Metacritic 85–88, over a million sold.

### 1.1 What it does

- **Three modes that gate each other.**
  - **The adventure:** one route per protagonist, in episodes of 20–30 minutes, played in any order. A route can lock
    until another character's route reaches a scene, a battle is won, an archive entry opens, or overall progress passes
    a mark. Each route has a flowchart with hints for its locks.
  - **The battle mode:** real-time-with-pause defence of a terminal against waves. Pilots on the bench guard the
    terminal; a fatigue meter benches pilots who fight too often.
  - **The archive:** wiki-style entries, some opened by the story and some bought with points from top battle ranks;
    plus a timeline of every scene, browsable by place, loop and character, and replayable.
- **It opens at the end.** The tutorial is the final battle, so the mystery is how, not whether.
- **Keywords.** Words picked up in talk and items can be thought about or used on people. Used in another route, they
  open branches.
- **How the twists are planted:**
  - contradictions between routes are deliberate clues;
  - a character who looks like a villain in one route gets context in her own;
  - small oddities pile up (a different surname in a note, shared dreams of wars never fought);
  - each route reads as a familiar sci-fi genre story, so players file every oddity under genre convention.
- **Cast balance.** Each protagonist gets a self-contained route that ends on the same beat, and appears as support in
  the others.

### 1.2 What players said

- **Praised:** the story, the structure and the art.
- **Criticised:** the battles, as repetitive and detached from the story; too much reading for the play; vague lock
  hints; a prologue that splices battles between introductions; thirteen routes open at once.

### 1.3 What the game takes

| Take | How it lands here | Call |
|---|---|---|
| The ending first | Each act opens with a **flash-forward**: one mission from the act's climax, with a fixed squad, as the act's tutorial | TC32 |
| Modes that gate each other | Each act has **Operations** (the spine: Gate incursions, light on story) and **Routes** (chapters led by one Uma). Operations need route progress; route chapters need operations; the act's last operation needs every route | TC32 |
| One route per lead | **Six route leads an act,** three or four open at once, each told through its own cyberpunk genre lens (heist, noir, idol scandal, street-racing crew, ghost-in-the-net, smuggling run) and ending on a shared beat | TC33 |
| Keywords | **Leads,** earned in scenes and battle side objectives, offered as one-tap choices. No hunting, no rewinds | TC34 |
| The archive | **The Archive** in the Hideout: Files on people, districts, factions and Gate-worlds with redacted lines that fill in; a Timeline of scenes; **Anomalies**, the contradictions the player has seen, open until explained | TC34 |
| Villains with context | Freeing a heroine opens her File and a short log from her side | TC34, WD12 |
| Fatigue | **Strain** benches an Uma after a run of missions; the bench still earns shared XP and gives one support call a battle | TC27 |
| Seeding | Three seeds per reveal across two routes; one deliberate contradiction an act; clues in the HUD, the Hideout's AI and the news tickers | WD18 |

**What we leave:** its twist pieces (simulated eras, clones, an ark, loops and memory wipes, avatars, one woman in three
bodies), every term it coined, thirteen leads at once, rewinds and hidden trigger spots, abstract wave battles, and its
fan service.

## 2. Star Guardian (cosmology and the squad)

Riot's skin universe across League of Legends, Wild Rift, Legends of Runeterra and Teamfight Tactics: the first team in
2015–16 with its short film, a veteran team in 2017 with a co-op mode, the return of a destroyed first team in 2019, and
a new generation in 2022 with a visual novel and a comic.

### 2.1 What it does

- **A cosmic power source** grants fragments of itself to chosen people with strong will. The power is a pact: a
  guardian fights until she dies or falls, and fear, doubt and lost hope make her fall.
- **A darkness** from outside feeds on fear and doubt. Its soldiers are void creatures, and small pockets of it open
  around the city.
- **The fallen.** A guardian who falls becomes the darkness's monster; another who broke over it hunts guardians to end
  the pact. Puppeted former teammates hunt the survivor. An infiltrator poses as a teacher and tempts the member who
  doubts.
- **Teams** of five with a captain, a mentor and small companions. By day they're students, with one civilian who knows
  the secret. Rival teams distrust each other, then ally against something bigger.
- **Themes:** duty against freedom (nearly every antagonist is a guardian who rejected the oath), the cost of power,
  found family, legacy and inherited trauma, doubt against hope, idols and pop music.
- **The look:** magical girl meets space opera. A shared base outfit with one signature colour per member, star gems,
  sparkle. The fallen keep their silhouettes in corrupted palettes and switch between forms mid-fight. The art stays
  candy-bright over a tragic story.

### 2.2 The arc shapes, by era

| Era | Shape |
|---|---|
| First team | The captain gathers the team; one walks out; the broken team is overwhelmed; she returns and the bond is stronger |
| Veterans | A rival team of veterans, an uneasy alliance, the question of who leads |
| The past returns | The destroyed first team; puppeted comrades hunt the survivor; the old captain saves her |
| New generation | Inherited trauma; the enemy inside the school; the fallen as victims; redemption |

### 2.3 What the game takes

| Take | How it lands here | Call |
|---|---|---|
| A power source as a pact | The squad's rigs run on **cores** stolen from the Authority's siphon programme; the power is the squad's original sin | WD1, WD3 |
| A corrupting darkness | **The Hollow Tide** in the dark between Gates, which the Authority bargains with; its backflow corrupts | WD2 |
| The fallen and the redeemed | Heroines arrive **corrupted** (an anchor part on the rig), **controlled** (a collar part) or **deceived** (no part), and each can be freed | TC26, WD9 |
| Double life | The Hideout sits behind a **front business** in the neon markets, kept by a civilian who knows; scenes by day, missions by night | TC39 |
| Transformation | **Suit-up:** the rig's parts snap on, each lit in the Uma's colour, at the start of a battle (skippable) and at big story moments | TC39 |
| A rival team | **The Authority's public hero squad,** idol-famous, on overclocked cores that burn their pilots out, led by a captain who knows the truth and believes in it | TC40, WD6 |
| The arc shapes | One an act: Act I forms the squad and one member walks out and returns; Act II is a rival squad, then an alliance; Act III's past returns; Act IV is a deceiver in plain sight; Act V is the redemption | WD16 |
| One colour per member | One hue per Uma on a shared graphite-and-white rig base, carried into her timeline icon, move tint and damage numbers | TC41 |

**What we leave:** every Riot name and term, the star gems and tiaras, star wands, cat familiars, the white-and-gold
bodices, and every plot beat (a team wiped out by a former teammate, the teacher villain, the fallen friend with her
companion caged inside, the fake defection, the trophy belt).

## 3. Front Mission (the battle)

Square's tactics series: Front Mission 1 (1995; *1st: Remake* 2022), 2 (1997; remake 2023), 3 (1999; remake 2025),
4 (2003) and 5 (2005, Japan only), plus 2089 on mobile and DS. *Evolved* is a shooter and *Left Alive* a stealth game;
neither applies.

### 3.1 What it does

- **Wanzers are built from parts:** body, left arm, right arm, legs, each with its own HP.
  - Body at 0: the unit is out (in the first game the pilot walks away; you pay for a new body).
  - One arm at 0: that arm's weapons are gone for the battle. Both arms at 0: no attack, no guard.
  - Legs at 0: movement drops to about one tile and evasion collapses.
  - After a battle, arms and legs are mended free; only a wrecked body costs money.
- **Targeting is set by the weapon,** and mostly random: melee leans to the body, machine guns and shotguns spread
  across parts, single shots hit one part. Choosing a part needs a skill, and the 2023 remake's lack of it was its top
  complaint.
- **Weight against output:** a wanzer's total weight can't pass its body's power output; backpacks add output or carry
  items, jump jets, sensors, repair kits or radios. In the first game a lighter load gives extra movement.
- **Weapons in three bands:** melee (one hit), short range (multi-hit, can counter) and long range (long reach,
  limited ammo, a minimum range, can't be countered and can't counter).
- **Damage types** in 4 and 5: piercing, impact and fire, against an armour coat of one type.
- **Turns:** phases in 1 to 3; Action Points from 2 on (moving, attacking, countering and blocking spend one pool,
  so a unit that spends everything on its turn is defenceless on the enemy's).
- **Links:** allies join an attack or take a hit for a partner. Setting them up in 4 was a chore; 5 simplified it.
- **Pilot skills** trigger by chance (a second hit, firing the other hand, striking first, stunning, choosing a part).
  In 3 they're learned at random by fighting with parts that carry them.
- **Capture:** in 3 a pilot forced out of her machine can board any empty one, so enemy machines can be taken.
- **Between battles:** towns with shops, a setup screen, an arena with odds, simulators and, in 3, a pseudo-internet.
- **Presentation:** every exchange cuts to a side-view duel that shows parts taking hits.

### 3.2 What players said

- **Loved:** customisation, part damage that makes each fight specific, links in 4 and 5, mission variety.
- **Frustrating:** slow pacing (unit counts, AP bookkeeping, passive AI that waits for scripts), hidden hit modifiers,
  chance skills that decide fights, no part choice, menu weight (no saved loadouts, part-by-part swaps for a dozen units),
  manual link setup, and a fast mode in the 2025 remake that hid the part damage.

### 3.3 What the game takes

| Take | How it lands here | Call |
|---|---|---|
| Wanzer parts | **The combat rig:** Core, Left arm, Right arm and Legs break; the Visor and the Back unit are slots that can't be hit | TC17 |
| Part-break effects | Front Mission 1's rules, plus spill-over: hits on a broken part go to the Core | TC17 |
| Weight against output | **Capacity:** every part has a Load of 1–6; Capacity comes from Power, the Core and the Back unit; a light load gives +1 Move | TC18 |
| Three weapon bands | Melee, Short and Long, with ammo only on Long, plus a fourth band, **Tech,** for a Netrunner's hacks | TC19 |
| Damage types | **P, I and F** (pierce, impact, fire); a **Coat** halves one | TC19 |
| Part targeting | Each weapon sets where hits land; **Aim** picks a part at −25% hit. The forecast lights the likely parts | TC19 |
| AP's spend-or-keep choice | Folded into **Charge**: counters and links need it, so spending it all leaves the Uma open | TC20 |
| Links | **Crossfire:** up to two allies join automatically; a shielded neighbour can take a hit. A toggle per unit, nothing to set up | TC21 |
| Skills from parts | **Mastery,** not chance: a rarer part teaches its skill as it's used; mastered skills go in Visor slots | TC22 |
| Chance skills | Five only, with the % always shown, and one chain per attack | TC22 |
| Jobs (Front Mission 5) | The five **roles** become jobs whose signature action needs its Back unit | TC5 |
| Pilot walks away | **Rig Break:** the Uma is out of the battle, never hurt; a Medic can reboot her in time; a broken Core costs credits | TC25 |
| Capture | **Jack-Out:** a Netrunner frees a weakened heroine with her parts intact | TC26 |
| Buy, salvage, remodel | Credits, salvage and scrap; a small bonus for three parts from one maker; free repainting | TC43 |
| Arena | A two-minute one-on-one on a 6 × 6 map with odds and a payout cap a chapter | TC43 |
| The cutaway | **Cut-ins** at three speeds (Full, Quick, Off), with part damage always shown on the map too | TC28 |

**What we leave:** AP arithmetic, hidden modifiers (night, weather, friendly-fire lines), random skill learning,
link setup screens, uncapped arena money, backpacks with no clear job, and Front Mission's own words (wanzer and the
rest). We say rig, Core, Back unit, Rig Break and Jack-Out.

## 4. Triangle Strategy (the look and the build-out)

Square Enix and Artdink, 2022, Unreal Engine 4. Metacritic 83–85, over a million sold.

### 4.1 What it does

- **The look.** HD-2D on larger, block-built maps than Octopath, with a camera that turns a full circle (which made every
  map edge a cost). Sprites were tuned to read as pixel art at more realistic proportions. Depth of field, bloom,
  vignette and chromatic aberration; reviewers found the image cleaner with depth of field off. Rain leaves puddles,
  snow melts, wind lowers arrow accuracy. Octopath's published recipe adds: deep depth of field so near and far stay
  sharp, point lights that cast moving shadows, a screen-space fade on anything in front of a character, pulsing
  emissive masks.
- **Battle UX.** A turn-order bar; tile colours (blue reachable and safe, purple reachable in enemy range, red lines
  from each enemy that could hit, red attack range, yellow area); a forecast of HP, damage and hit chance for the
  attack and the follow-up; a simulation of out-of-range enemies; a terrain card; an autosave every turn and a retry.
- **Battle systems.** Each unit's clock fills by Speed; a turn is a move and an action; waiting brings the next turn
  sooner. **TP:** +1 a turn, a cap of 3 rising with promotions, skills cost it, basic attacks are free. **Follow-ups:**
  an ally on the far side of the target attacks too. Back attacks always crit; height adds damage and archer range.
  **Terrain:** fire melts ice into puddles, lightning spreads across wet tiles and metal track; houses collapse; wind
  pushes. **Kudos** from back attacks and follow-ups buy once-a-battle **Quietuses**. No permadeath; retreat keeps XP.
- **Between battles.** **The Scales of Conviction:** seven companions vote with tokens on key choices, after the hero
  tries to persuade each; some arguments only unlock with what exploration found. Three hidden convictions move with
  dialogue and how you fight, and decide some recruits and the ending. **Exploration** of the next battlefield.
  **The Encampment:** a provisioner, a smithy that ranks up weapons, a trade post and **mock battles** that give XP even
  on a loss.

### 4.2 What players said

- **Praised:** the map design, follow-ups and scarce TP, how the votes tie into the story, the art.
- **Criticised:** an hour or more of cutscenes between battles and a slow opening; story chapters that are all
  cutscene; convictions that feel predictable; blur on Switch; rooftop archers that trivialise maps.

### 4.3 What the game takes

| Take | How it lands here | Call |
|---|---|---|
| The HD-2D diorama | uma-stage's fixed 35° camera with no rotation in v1; a wide sharp band of tilt-shift in battle, a stronger one in story shots; bloom only on neon, flashes and holograms; night by default | TC10 |
| Speed clocks | Kept: the Speed timeline across the top | TC3 |
| TP | **Charge:** +1 a turn, cap 3, up to 5 with licences | TC20 |
| Follow-ups | **Crossfire,** extended to Gunners with a line of fire, since a squad of four rarely sandwiches a target | TC21 |
| Back attacks and height | Side +25%, back +50% and past shields and counters; +10% a height level; every high perch has an answer | TC23 |
| Elemental terrain | **Terrain states:** Wet, Oil (and its fire), Smoke, Live current, plus **terminals** a Netrunner can use | TC24 |
| Tile colours and forecast | The **forecast card** and range colours, with a toggle that paints every enemy's reach | TC29 |
| Quietus | **Overrides:** a squad pool, each once a battle, bought with Rep | TC44 |
| Convictions and the vote | **The Call:** the squad votes with tokens; three convictions (**Code, Edge, Free**) are visible, not hidden | TC35 |
| Exploration | **Intel scenes:** one district diorama, four to six hotspots, about three minutes | TC35 |
| The Encampment | **The Hideout:** Workshop, Shop, Fixer, Sim Pod, Crew and Archive | TC42 |
| Mock battles | **The Sim Pod:** replays story maps with new enemies, XP for the whole squad even on a loss | TC42 |

**What we leave:** the 360° camera, armies and hour-long battles, hidden convictions, fixed weapons, loot bags that
cost a turn, and long cutscene runs: a scene is three minutes at most (TC36).

an original name, a reason to be on the Ring, a way to turn and a battle identity. [The world](world.md) holds the full

the owner ranks.

| Genshin Impact | Boss | 56 | Elements that react on tiles |

## 6. What the sibling games already settled

The pass over uma-quest, uma-farm and uma-platform set rules this game follows:

- **The cast is the Ranker's 100,** one per outfit, in its ten batches of ten (uma-platform P131, P138, P139). The base
  outfit is the real Uma; sisters are Umas of their own; F means unused. Umas are keyed by outfit, never by character
  id alone, because a sister shares her base Uma's character id.
- **Roles come from an authored tag,** not the running style. uma-quest moved its Ways to a hand-authored story class
  (its SC7, D50) and uma-farm its favourite line to the bible (RM6), after the owner said he was moving away from the class
  separation (P123). Running style only colours how she moves.
- **Stats:** both games read the five stats per outfit. Every outfit's 5★ stats sum to 550, so only the spread differs.
- **Bosses come from the boards' casts** in authoring order, the owner picks, the tools never rank, left-out units are
  never used, and tiers are never game rules (TS14). uma-quest fills encounter slots (Regulars by role, one Key, one
  Boss) from pools of board ids (its SP43).
- **Umas are adults and are never maimed.** A damaged pose is weary with the costume intact.
- **Stories** go through Uma Story's draft, review and publish (P141). A fourth book needs its own P-decision.
- **Art:** the Combat Adult Character keys are side-on and face one way (uma-quest ships 12 a Uma, never mirrored, in
  its own private pack, D45). The chibi sets walk in four facings.
- **uma-stage today:** an orthographic camera pitched 35° with a fixed yaw, one texel per device pixel; a grid builder
  with heights 0–2; pure tile picking and A*; tilt-shift and bloom that the battery saver turns off by default on phones;
  at most 8 live sprite sets. It has no range overlays, no tactical camera, no rotation, no emissive glow and no rain
  ([the concept §7](concept.md#7-how-its-built-tc10-tc11-tc14-tc48)).
