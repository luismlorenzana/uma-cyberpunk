# Life aboard: the research

> **Status: 2026-11-13 (D20).** Three research passes ran in parallel for the owner's new direction
> ([life-aboard.md](life-aboard.md)): **how games build and live on a ship or mobile base**, **how games assign named
> characters to duties**, and **how games do relationships, light romance, decorated quarters and bond-driven combat**.
> Each read official pages, wikis, reviews and design talks. Several wikis (Fandom, GamePress, Koumakan, Prydwen) blocked
> direct reads, so some facts come from their search extracts, cross-checked where possible; numbers in live games
> change with patches, so treat them as snapshots. Anything not verified is said so. The design's calls are TC55–TC74.

## The short answer

- **Ships:** the strongest patterns are **one visible budget** (FTL's reactor, XCOM 2's power, Starfield's classes),
  **a base that grows as people join** (Suikoden, Skies of Arcadia), **crew at stations giving ship-wide effects** (FTL,
  Skies of Arcadia), and **the ship in battle as a few capped calls** (Valkyria Chronicles 4's Ship Orders, the closest
  model to this game). The payoffs: **upgrades tested at a finale** (Mass Effect 2) and **your own ship as a battle map**
  (XCOM 2's Avenger Defense). Avoid upkeep, gathering minigames, maintenance that punishes, and walking hubs.
- **Duties:** **rooms keyed to one stat** are the most legible match (Fallout Shelter, Uma Musume's own training
  facilities). Real-time timers draw the most complaints in single-player games (Dragon Age: Inquisition's war table).
  Every base that launched without **auto-assign and presets** added them later (Arknights, Girls' Frontline, Metal Gear
  Solid V). **Thresholds beat sums** for keeping spreadsheets away.
- **Bonds:** **a ranked meter with scenes at thresholds** (Persona, Fire Emblem, NIKKE), fed by **time, gifts and fighting
  together**, paying out in battle as **small stats, reactive moments or shared actions** (Persona's Follow Up and Endure,
  XCOM 2's bonds, Chrono Trigger's techs). **Romance as a branch at the top of friendship**, with **equal rewards**
  (Dragon Age II, Fire Emblem Engage's platonic S-supports), avoids the pressure to romance. **Decoration is a comfort
  score** feeding a passive benefit (Blue Archive, Arknights, Azur Lane). The pitfalls: tone that ages badly, gift grind,
  jealousy, and punishing neglect, which with a hundred crew becomes a hundred chores.

## 1. Ships and mobile bases

| Game | What it does | What players said | What this game takes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FTL** | One reactor powers the systems; scrap buys reactor bars and system levels; a crew member manning a system boosts it and gains skill by doing it | The trade-offs are the game; the complaint is randomness | One budget in pips (TC62); stations that boost systems (TC67) |
| **Mass Effect 2** | Seven Normandy upgrades; three of them decide who dies in the finale; crew talks lead to loyalty missions | The finale's payoff is famous; mineral scanning was panned; the cabin fish die if unfed | Readiness before a finale, without deaths (TC64); no maintenance punishments |
| **Mass Effect 3** | War assets from every recruit and favour add up to one strength meter that gates the endings | One visible meter collecting a whole campaign | Faction standing and operations feeding one readiness (TC70, TC71) |
| **XCOM 2** | Twelve rooms in a cross-section; engineers dig and staff them; power from relays; the Avenger is defended as a mission | Engineers are the bottleneck; several rooms are traps | The cross-section on one screen (TC62); boarding missions (TC65); no trap modules |
| **Skies of Arcadia** | Twenty-two crew recruited into ship roles, each a fixed passive; turn-based ship battles; the home base grows as crew arrive | Beloved; the battles too frequent | Station chiefs (TC67); the ship grows as crew join (TC49) |
| **Suikoden II and IV** | The castle (in IV a five-deck ship) levels up at recruit thresholds; each recruit opens a service | Watching the base grow is the series' signature; IV's slow sailing hurt | Rooms open with leads (TC62) |
| **Starfield** | A builder with one reactor, class limits and mass; power split in flight; crew skills apply to the ship | The builder is its most praised system; space is mostly fast travel | Classes as module levels; per-sortie power (TC62) |
| **Spiritfarer** | Buildings on a grid aboard a boat; a house for each passenger; boat upgrades open sea routes | The cosy home loved; the requests called busywork | A cabin per Uma (TC60); requests kept light (TC61); capability opens routes (TC66) |
| **Honkai: Star Rail** | The train as the hub between worlds; a room to decorate added later | Players long asked for housing | The ship as home between worlds |
| **Final Fantasy's airships** | Getting the airship opens the world; later games make it the party's base | — | The ship opens the map by capability (TC66) |
| **Star Traders: Frontiers** | Component slots, crew jobs filling ship roles, installs taking days | Deep, but heavy and long | Keep it light |
| **Space Haven, Cosmoteer** | Tile-by-tile ships with simulated needs and walking distances | Deep and creative; not for short sessions | Not this |
| **Sunless Skies** | Locomotive slots, officers with stories, fuel and terror on the way | The writing praised; the early resource squeeze not | Events in transit, without the squeeze (TC66) |
| **Starsector** | Ordnance points per ship; supplies spent to deploy | — | No upkeep |
| **Fire Emblem Engage** | A floating hub whose rewards renew per battle; a cook's meal buffs the army | Optional and calm, but thin on bonding and a walk to every facility | Per-battle rewards and meals (TC62, TC67); one screen, no walking |
| **Fire Emblem: Three Houses** | A walkable monastery on activity points; cooking and teaching raise stats and bonds | Rich, then repetitive | No walking hub, no activity points |
| **Battlestar Galactica Deadlock** | A campaign map of fleets and raids | The campaign layer fiddly | Keep travel a tap |
| **Valkyria Chronicles 4** | The land cruiser's Ship Orders in a turn-based battle: radar, cannon fire, repair, rescue, capped per battle, grown by R&D | — | The model for ship support Orders (TC59, TC64) |
| **BattleTech** | Argo upgrades raise morale and repairs, and raise the monthly bill | The modules expensive; ruin should only lurk | No upkeep (TC62) |
| **Arknights** | Power plants power the rooms; staff morale drains and recovers in dorms with furniture | Strong progression, but a timer chore | Not the timers |

**Patterns:**
1. One budget with visible pips forces readable trade-offs.
2. The base grows as people join.
3. Crew on stations give ship-wide effects.
4. The ship shows up in battle as a few capped calls.
5. Upgrades pay off at a finale.
6. Upgrades open the map.
7. Rewards refresh per battle, not per hour.
8. The ship itself as a battle map.
9. Personal touches make it home.

**Pitfalls:** real-time timers and dispatch chores; upkeep that becomes a clock; gathering minigames to pay for upgrades;
maintenance punishments; walking hubs and action points; trap modules; slow travel and forced encounters on the way;
simulation depth that needs long sessions.

## 2. Crew duties

| Game | What it does | What players said | What this game takes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Arknights** | Rooms staffed by operators with unique base skills; morale drains (about 1 an hour) and recovers in dorms; real time | Optimizers love it; swaps called a chore; presets, quick-switch and a third-party bot followed; the sequel shipped auto-rotating shifts | Presets and auto-assign from the start; no duty morale (TC68) |
| **Fallout Shelter** | Each room keyed to one stat; output the sum of workers' stat; dragging shows a dweller's contribution; real-time cycles | Clear and tactile; "middle management" and nagging notifications | Stat-keyed posts with a preview (TC67) |
| **XCOM 2, War of the Chosen** | Engineers staff rooms; covert actions send named soldiers away for days, with risks; tired soldiers risk traits | The Workshop not worth it | Operations without catastrophic risk (TC70) |
| **FTL** | Skill at a station grows only by doing it | A combat decision, never a chore | Post mastery (TC67) |
| **Fire Emblem: Three Houses** | Weekly lectures on motivation; paired group tasks raising skills and supports | The weekly loop tedious on repeat | Duty partners (TC68) |
| **Fire Emblem Engage** | Café meals buff the army; resources respawn after every battle | Unhurried; also repetitive collecting | Meals; nothing to collect by hand |
| **Azur Lane** | Commissions lock fleets away for hours; the dorm's comfort feeds EXP | — | No locks, no hours |
| **Girls' Frontline, NIKKE** | Logistics lock squads away for up to a day; NIKKE's outpost idles | Auto-repeat added later | The soft cost instead of a lock (TC68) |
| **Darkest Dungeon** | Stress relief buildings; a week passes per expedition; side effects like going missing | — | Strain relief rooms (Med Bay, Observation Deck); no failure rolls |
| **Suikoden, Skies of Arcadia** | Recruits bring a service or a fixed ship perk; no management | Beloved; collecting the crew is the content | Station chiefs (TC67) |
| **Honkai: Star Rail, Genshin Impact** | Dispatches of hours; characters stay usable | A thirty-second daily claim, no decision | Not this |
| **RimWorld, Oxygen Not Included** | Standing work priorities; passions speed growth; traits block jobs | Deep, for long sessions | Standing posts that persist (TC68) |
| **Final Fantasy XIV** | Retainer ventures of an hour or eighteen | Rewards checking in hourly | Not this |
| **Metal Gear Solid V** | Staff ranks per department; one auto-assign button | "Runs on auto-pilot", yet personal | Auto-assign (TC68) |
| **Dragon Age: Inquisition** | War-table missions on real-time timers | Called a free-to-play carry-over; modded to instant | No real time, ever (TC67) |
| **Final Fantasy Tactics** | Errands for days on the world map, with a recommended job | Odds opaque | Watches, with the math shown |
| **Mass Effect 2 and 3** | The finale's role picks; war assets gating the endings | — | Readiness (TC64) |
| **Persona 5** | Part-time jobs spend a time slot for money and stats | — | — |
| **Uma Musume** | Five training facilities, one per stat, each levelling with use; failure below half energy | — | Posts keyed to the five bands; post mastery (TC67) |

**Patterns:**
1. Rooms keyed to a stat are the most legible match.
2. Unique per-character perks give named characters a life outside battle, and cause most of the min-maxing.
3. Opportunity cost runs from none (Genshin) to a hard lock (Girls' Frontline); the middle is best.
4. Recovery as a room gives the bench a purpose.
5. Three time bases (real time, in-game time, per battle); real time draws the most complaints.
6. Growth by doing the job.
7. Pairing at a task builds relationships.
8. Duty output often buys a one-battle edge.
9. Automation added after launch shows where the chore was.
10. Thresholds beat linear sums.

**Pitfalls:** real-time timers and appointments; collecting by hand; a second fatigue meter needing rotation; synergy
webs that need spreadsheets; dispatch with no cost, or a lock that steals the squad; activities detached from the
characters; a fixed hub loop that drags; hidden math; punishing failure on passive duty.

## 3. Bonds, romance and quarters

| Game | What it does | What players said | What this game takes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sakura Wars** | The commander walks the theater; timed dialogue choices move Trust, which raises stats in the chapter's battle; pair attacks; one heroine's ending | The theater hub loved; the 2019 bath and close-up scenes called dated | The captain's role; tone guardrails (TC72) |
| **Fire Emblem** | Supports C to S from fighting side by side and social time; combat bonuses per supporting ally; one S-support; Engage's platonic S-supports | Three Houses' writing praised; tea time a quiz; Fates' petting minigame cut | Bond scenes at ranks; equal paths (TC56, TC57) |
| **Persona 3–5** | Ranks 1–10 on a calendar; ranks grant Follow Up, Endure, Protect; romance chosen at rank 9; jealousy in some games, later toned down | — | Reactive perks, Endure at rank 8 (TC58); no jealousy |
| **XCOM 2, War of the Chosen** | Pair bonds from shared missions and moments; one bond each; extra actions and Dual Strike | Bonds from play, not scripts | Partners (TC58) |
| **Mass Effect 2** | Loyalty missions unlock powers; disloyal crew die in the finale; romance separate and exclusive | The payoff celebrated; neglect a permanent penalty | Personal operations, without the penalty (TC57) |
| **Blue Archive** | The never-shown Sensei; relationship ranks add small stats and unlock stories; the café's furniture comfort and invitations; MomoTalk messages | The warmth praised; the intimacy branding criticized | The captain never drawn (TC55); messages; comfort (TC56, TC61) |
| **Azur Lane** | Affection from sorties and the dorm; stat tiers; several oaths allowed | Very slow increments | Non-exclusive top tier |
| **NIKKE** | Advise: a right answer full, a wrong one half; bond levels with five episodes; gifts uncapped | Players dump gifts and skip the episodes | Half for a wrong answer, no fail state; one gift a visit (TC61) |
| **Arknights** | Trust raises stats; dorm ambience from furniture speeds morale recovery | — | Comfort sheds Strain (TC61) |
| **Genshin Impact** | The teapot's furniture load and a score rewarding quantity; companions with favourite sets | — | Six slots so clutter never pays; sets (TC61) |
| **Honkai: Star Rail** | No meter: companion missions, then visits and phone messages | — | Messages after battles |
| **Star Ocean** | Private actions move friendship and romance values; ninety-nine endings | Easy to miss | Nothing missable; epilogue cards |
| **Dragon Age** | Approval and gifts (Origins); Friendship or Rivalry, both rewarded, in II | Origins' loop called exploitative; II's equal rewards the fix | Equal paths (TC56) |
| **Stardew Valley** | Gift limits, heart events, decay, jealousy events | — | No decay, no jealousy |
| **Spiritfarer** | A house per passenger; requests; one character hates improvements | The houses express each character | Requests; contrarians (TC61) |
| **Animal Crossing, The Sims** | Rooms scored by items, sets and themes | — | Sets (TC61) |
| **Chrono Trigger** | Dual and triple techs between party members | — | Duo techniques (TC58) |
| **Hades** | Gifts give keepsakes; several romances with no jealousy | — | Non-exclusive romance |

**Patterns:**
1. A ranked meter with scenes at thresholds.
2. Three inputs: time together, gifts by preference, fighting side by side.
3. Five strengths of battle payoff, from small stats to a unique power.
4. Gates: a story chapter, a personal mission, a key item.
5. Romance as a branch at the top of friendship.
6. Decoration as a comfort score capped by a facility's level.
7. Matching her taste.
8. A time budget per cycle.
9. A messaging app for cheap contact.
10. Endings that reflect bonds.

**Pitfalls:** tone that ages badly, and a captain courting subordinates; grind and gift spam; romance worth more than
friendship; jealousy mechanics; punishing neglect; missable scenes; a writing budget of a hundred crew times their
scenes.

## 4. What the game takes

| Finding | Where it lands |
|---|---|
| The never-drawn, player-named point of view (Blue Archive's Sensei) | TC55 |
| Ranks with scenes at thresholds, gated by the story; no decay, nothing missable; catch-up below the median | TC56, TC57 |
| She makes the first move at rank 8; Trust and Heart equal in power; a Romance setting; epilogue cards | TC56, TC72 |
| Small, reactive, once-a-battle perks; Endure; one Resonance a battle; partners from play with a duo technique | TC58 |
| The ship's Orders, Valkyria Chronicles 4's model; readiness at a finale, Mass Effect 2's without deaths | TC59, TC64 |
| A cabin per Uma; six slots so only her best items count; sets; tastes discovered; theme kits; requests; Comfort sheds Strain | TC60, TC61 |
| One screen, no walking; rooms open with leads; three visible levels; no upkeep; no trap modules | TC62 |
| Install everything, power a few battle systems per sortie | TC62, TC64 |
| Your own ship as a battle map | TC65 |
| Capability opens the map; one-tap event cards | TC66 |
| Posts keyed to a band by thresholds; running style as how she settles in; mastery by doing; station chiefs | TC67 |
| Automation from the start; one ship-log card; no duty fatigue; no failure rolls; a soft cost to deploying | TC68 |
| Operations as war assets without catastrophe; one readiness for the finale | TC70, TC71 |
