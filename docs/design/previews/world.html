Uma Cyberpunk: the world

Status: 2026-11-13, the world v2 (D28): the story's base elements, for the owner's read. The owner asked to start the story with full worldbuilding, around six notes: an interplanetary adventure exploring cities on planets with different environments; ship travel and time with the captain at base camp; ship maintenance and each character's aptitudes; ship-to-ship combat; team combat on the ground with cybernetic weapons; and boarding enemy ships. This page answers them, and the second story brief's prompts (SB13: the big plot, the factions in depth, the ship, the captain, the squads, life aboard, the relationships), on top of the first pass (D3, D4), whose truth it keeps.

  • Calls: WD1–WD24 from the first pass, each marked kept, amended or superseded, and WD25–WD48, new. Each is a recommendation, adopted until the owner changes it.
  • Nameless (WD22): no Uma, no heroine, nothing from a franchise beyond its title. Places, ships, factions and squads have our own names. Who leads which world, which heroine leads which faction, and every line are the story's private files: the casting and the first two worlds' bibles are written (D30), read in the Story Sandbox's Bibles page.
  • Read it here, on its page, or world by world in the Story Sandbox (pnpm stories), where each world has a verdict box.
  • What the game is asked for is in §14 and the handoff's story requests: ship battles, boarding enemy ships, maintenance and walkable cities are new to the game.

The short answer

  • The Circuit: the Ring and seven worlds, each a different environment with one city to walk, joined by the Lanes (WD25, WD27). The worlds are an orbital megacity, an ocean, a jungle, a forge world, a gas giant, an ice moon, a dead orbital ring and a glass desert at the edge of the dark (WD28).
  • The Tithe is the spine: the Ring glows on power its Authority siphons from every other world in the Circuit. Each world the ship visits shows what's being drained from it: tides, growth, heat, storms, light (WD1).
  • The Blackout hunts between the worlds and eats memory. The Authority's board bargains with it (WD2).
  • The ship is the Homestretch: the first ship that ever went through a Gate, scrapped in the Ring's Docks and stolen back in the first chapter. It's the crew's home, base camp and stronghold (WD30).
  • The Steward, the ship's mind, lost most of its memory to the Blackout. Repairing the ship brings it back, so maintenance is also how the mystery unfolds (WD31, WD33).
  • The captain is "unwritten": the one person the Blackout can't read. The ship flies for them, and the crew learns to trust them (WD36).
  • The Umas are the Gatechildren: twenty years ago the Homestretch came back through the first Gate carrying them as children. The Authority scattered them across the Circuit and scrapped the ship. Each grew up on a different world, which is why the ship finds its leads one world at a time (WD37).
  • Bonds are the weapon the Blackout can't answer: a memory two people share is held twice, and it can't be eaten whole. The power of friendship and the power of love are the same power, literally (WD41).
  • Three ways to fight: in Frames on the ground (WD42, WD43), from the posts in the Lanes (WD44), and in the corridors of a boarded ship (WD45). Heroines are freed, never killed; a beaten ship strikes, drifts or flees, never explodes with its crew.
  • Five acts across the eight worlds, one layer of the truth each, ending back at the Ring's Gate Heart (WD47).

2. The Circuit (WD25–WD27)

WD25, the Circuit. One star system: the Ring and seven worlds, joined by the Lanes, the routes ships jump along. The Lanes run in four regions, each opened by the ship's capability (TC66): the Homestretch can't reach the edge until it has been rebuilt for it.

WD26, two kinds of Gate.

  • Siphon Gates sit inside the Circuit, one at each world. They're the Tithe: rings of light with a gap in them, each in its world's colour, humming while they drain. Every one feeds the Gate Heart in the Ring.
  • Outer Gates open to other universes. The eight factions came through them: expeditions, mercenaries, the lost and the bound.
  • The factions' home universes stay beyond the outer Gates and off the star map (SB3). The worlds are ours, and the factions come to them.
  • The first outer Gate the Authority ever opened is the one the Homestretch went through, twenty years ago (WD37).

WD27, what every world has:

  • one environment, distinct from every other;
  • one city to walk, with districts, contacts, shops and investigation leads, explored in the Street look;
  • one Tithe wound: what the siphon is draining, visible in the streets;
  • wild places beyond the city for ground missions;
  • space around it where ships fight;
  • one or two factions that are new there, so a world teaches at most two new mechanics (SB3);
  • one lead: an Uma who grew up there, whose recruitment arc brings her aboard (private casting);
  • something for the ship: a room or an upgrade, and a decor kit for the cabins (WD29).

The Steel Muster, the Authority's android foot soldiers, are on every world, so the regular-enemy board pays off when they defect (WD47).

3.1 The Ring (world 1, Act I)

  • Look: a megacity on an orbital ring, stacked in six layers (WD4, kept as this world's city): the Spires (the Authority's white towers), the Districts (neon streets, holo-ads, rain), the Markets (night markets and back rooms), the Underlevels (the dark under the city), the Docks (where ships leave for the Lanes) and the Gate Heart (where every siphon meets).
  • To walk: the Districts, the Markets, the Underlevels and the Docks. The Spires and the Gate Heart stay closed until the finale.
  • The Tithe: the Ring is its destination. It's the brightest place in the Circuit because every other world is getting darker.
  • Fights: the Muster and the Commission in the streets; in orbit, the Authority's patrol cutters hunting a stolen ship (the first ship battle, a breakout).
  • The arc: the first lead's recruitment arc. She, the captain and a handful of crew find the Homestretch in the scrapyard Docks and steal it out from under the Authority. Lens: a heist.
  • For the ship: the Bridge and the first squad.

3.2 Tidewell (world 2, Act II)

  • Look: an ocean world with no land, only reefs and storm-white lighthouses. Its city, the Moorings, floats on locks, pontoons and stilts in tiers: the Lock Market, the Lantern Piers, the Drydocks, and a Sunken Quarter under glass.
  • The Tithe: its moons' pull is siphoned off, so the tides are stopping. The sea goes still and stale, the locks stand dry, and the harbour that lived on the tides is starving.
  • Fights: the Tideline Armada's two blocs. One blockades the Moorings; the other holds a treaty with the Authority. Most of the world's battles are at sea and in orbit, against the blockade.
  • Boarding: the blockade's flagship, where the free bloc's admiral is held to the treaty. Boarding her ship wins the free bloc over.
  • The arc: a lead who grew up running the locks. Lens: a smuggling run.
  • For the ship: the Galley, and a seaside decor kit.

3.3 Verdance (world 3, Act II)

  • Look: a jungle in a gorge so deep it has its own weather, glowing blue-green at night. The city, the Terraces, is cut down the gorge walls: temple-greenhouses, canopy bridges, the Root Galleries underneath, and the Seed Vault at the bottom.
  • The Tithe: its growth. The forest is greying from the top of the gorge down, leaves turning to glassy ash.
  • Fights: the Sevenfold Envoys, an honour guard from a world of seven elements, searching the gorge for a lost companion. The Blackout speaks to them in their patron's voice (an Anchor). Their shrine terraces are battle maps where elements react.
  • The arc: a lead raised in the Terraces. Lens: a missing-persons search: the Envoys' lost companion, and the lead's own lost family.
  • For the ship: the Garden, grown from Verdance cuttings, and a greenhouse decor kit.

3.4 Anvil (world 4, the turn of Act II)

  • Look: a volcanic world of lava rivers under an ash sky. Its city, the Slipways, fills a caldera: foundries, shipyards, Frame-smiths, and the Cooling Quarter where people who can't afford heat crowd together.
  • The Tithe: its heat. The forges are cooling, and the city rations warmth by the hour.
  • A breather, and the ship's world. No new faction: Anvil is a free port where the Neon Commission trades and the Muster keeps a depot. It's where the Homestretch is refitted, where Frames are made, and where the Lab buys Data.
  • The ship's history surfaces: the Homestretch was built in these yards. The yard's old records, and the first of the Steward's memories restored here, are the first seeds of Act V (WD31).
  • Fights: raids on the slipways, including the first time someone boards the Homestretch (TC65), and a rival ship's crew.
  • The arc: a lead who grew up a Frame-smith's apprentice. Lens: a corporate thriller.
  • For the ship: Engineering, and an industrial decor kit.

3.5 Gale (world 5, Act III)

  • Look: a gas giant with no ground. Highfall is a city of platforms riding the storm currents, tied together by cable lifts: the Weather Exchange, the Underdecks, the Storm Shrine, the Tether Docks.
  • The Tithe: its storms. The bands are slowing; without the currents, the platforms sink a little lower every year.
  • Fights: the Starward Contractors, agents from other stars hired to guard Gale's siphon. They fight with typed shields and bend the timeline. The heaviest ship battles of the game happen here, in lightning and cloud.
  • Boarding: a Contractor frigate, to steal the contract itself and rewrite it.
  • The arc: a lead raised on the platforms. Lens: a noir case.
  • The layer: the Contractors' scanners read the crew's Frames, and the Homestretch's own heart, as siphon cores (WD3).
  • For the ship: the Sensor deck, and a sky decor kit.

3.6 Hush (world 6, Act IV)

  • Look: an ice moon under a black sky, where sound carries strangely and draws the static. Its city, the Vaults, is a run of geothermal halls under the ice. People speak softly there. Above them: the Bell Tower, the ice harbour and a frozen market.
  • The Tithe: nothing left to take. Hush was the first world drained, generations ago. It's what every world in the Circuit becomes.
  • Fights: the Lantern Wardens, survivors of a world that already ended, sheltering here, and their cult, the Tolling, who ring the Bell Tower to call the Blackout in.
  • Boarding: ghost ships frozen into the moon's ice rings.
  • The arc: a lead raised in the Vaults. Lens: a pirate broadcast.
  • For the ship: the Infirmary, and a hushed, snowlit decor kit.

3.7 The Hollow Ring (world 7, Act IV)

  • Look: the Ring's sister, dead. A derelict orbital city around a dead planet: dark Districts, drowned Spires, ad screens still flickering for nobody. It's what the Ring becomes when the Authority's bargain comes due.
  • Fights: the Iron Vigil, the crew's dark mirror (orbital soldiers in combat bodies), and the Transfigured, Vigil soldiers the Blackout has spread through like an infection.
  • Boarding is the main way in. The Hollow Ring is a derelict, taken section by section through airlocks, with helmets sealed.
  • The arc: a lead who grew up in a salvage crew working the ring's edge. Lens: survival.
  • For the ship: the Lab's last branch, and a salvage decor kit.

3.8 Brink (world 8, Act V)

  • Look: a glass desert at the edge of the Circuit, where the dark begins in the sky. The Last Oasis is a caravan city sheltering under the walls of the Ember Bastion's convents: the Caravanserai, the Glass Fields, the Convents and the Edge.
  • The Tithe: its sun, the hardest-drained of all. The light is thinning, and the sand is turning to glass.
  • Fights: the Ember Bastion, a sisterhood that has held the line against corruption all its life, and the Crowned, its strongest, whom the Blackout crowned as its generals.
  • Boarding: a Crowned's flagship.
  • The arc: the last lead, raised in a convent. Lens: a pilgrimage.
  • For the ship: the readiness for the last jump.

WD29, each world gives the ship something: a room or an upgrade (the Bridge, Galley, Garden, Engineering, Sensor deck, Infirmary and the Lab's last branch), a decor kit for the cabins (TC61), and a lead. The game's room plan (TC62) can take these as the rooms' story; any can move.

4. The ship: the Homestretch (WD30–WD35)

4.1 What it is (WD30, WD35)

  • WD30: the ship is the Homestretch, a long-hulled Gate-ship. Twenty years ago it was the first ship sent through an outer Gate. It came back carrying children and none of its crew. The Authority scrapped it in the Ring's Docks and buried the record. In Act I it's stolen back, and it becomes the crew's home, base camp and stronghold. Its name is the last stretch of a race, before the finish.
  • WD35, why it travels:
    • to gather the scattered Gatechildren, one world at a time;
    • to stop the Tithe;
    • and, though nobody aboard knows it at first, to go home: the Homestretch remembers the way back through the first

    Gate, to a world with a sunlit track.

4.2 The Steward, and the rooms (WD31, WD32)

  • WD31, the Steward: the ship's mind. It's warm, dry-humoured, protective, and full of holes, because the Blackout ate most of it in the twenty years in the scrapyard.
    • Repairing the ship restores it: every module brought back to level, and some repairs after battles, return a

    fragment of its memory, as a Log File (TC34).

    • The fragments are the seeds of the last layer: the voyage through the first Gate, the crew that didn't come back,

    the children in the hold.

    • The Steward never knows more than it has recovered, so it can be wrong, and it's one of the Anomalies (WD18).

4.3 Maintenance and aptitudes (WD33, WD34)

  • WD33, maintenance is care, not a chore. The Homestretch is twenty years scrapped and patched together.
    • It wears from what it does: jumps through damaged Lanes, ship battles, and being boarded. Each leaves damage on

    the modules that took it.

    • The crew repairs it at their posts over watches: a mission or a jump, never real time (TC67).
    • It never decays while it sits. There's no upkeep, no breakdown from neglect and no failure roll (TC68 kept).
    • A damaged module still works, just weaker in battle, until it's repaired.
    • Repair restores the Steward (WD31).
    • Taking time aboard to repair after a hard battle is a beat of life aboard, not a tax.
  • WD34, every Uma has aptitudes. Her stats and running style make her better at some posts than others (TC67's band thresholds), and she has a post she loves (a liked duty).
    • The ship's stations: the helm, the guns, the shields, engines, sensors, the Hangar's deck crew, the Infirmary,

    the Galley and the Garden.

    • In a ship battle (WD44), the crew at those posts is the ship's strength.
    • In repair (WD33), the right Uma at the right post fixes it faster.
    • Which Uma is good at what is the casting's, privately; the game derives it from her stats until then.

5. The captain (WD36)

WD36, the captain is "unwritten": the one person aboard the Blackout can't read.

  • The Steward won't fly for anyone it can't trust its memory with, and the captain is the only one whose past the Blackout has never touched. That's why the ship answers to them, though they've never commanded anything.
  • Who the captain was before is the player's to imagine. The game never shows their face, never fixes their past, and uses the pronouns the player picks (SB15).
  • The crew's view changes:
    • at first: a stranger the ship chose. Some Umas resent it; some are curious.
    • in time: the one person who remembers everything for them. When the Blackout takes a memory from an Uma, the

    captain still has it, and gives it back in a bond scene.

  • The captain gives Orders from the Bridge (TC59) and is at the heart of every bond (WD41). They never fight on the board.

6. The crew (WD37–WD39)

6.1 The Gatechildren (WD37)

WD37 amends WD8:

  • Where they came from: twenty years ago, the Homestretch came back through the first outer Gate with a hold full of sleeping children: the Umas, from a world with a sunlit track.
  • What the Authority did: feared what it had found, so it scattered them across the Circuit's worlds as foundlings, one or two to a world, and scrapped the ship.
  • What the Blackout did: trimmed their memories down to a dream of running under a sun they've never seen.
  • Now: they're adults, grown on different worlds into different lives, and they don't know about each other. That's why the Homestretch finds its leads one world at a time, Octopath Traveler's way.
  • What they share: the dream, a pull toward the ship they can't explain, and a way of running they never learned.
  • The crew is Umas only (SB3).

6.2 The squads (WD38)

WD38, up to four squads (TC69), each with a banner and a way of fighting. Who leads each is the casting's, privately. They're named the way the Umas joke, in words from a sport none of them remembers playing.

6.3 The rival squad (WD39)

WD39 amends WD6:

  • Who: the Authority's licensed heroes, the Laurel Guard, famous on every billboard in the Ring. They're Umas from the NPC board (TC40), Gatechildren too, raised by the Authority itself.
  • Their ship: the Laurel, a sleek hero ship that hunts the Homestretch across the Circuit.
  • Their cost: they fly on overclocked cores that burn their pilots out.
  • Their captain: knows the truth and believes the Ring's survival justifies it, so the rivalry is sincere.
  • Their arc: they're the first ship battle you can't win (the Ring); allies of convenience at the Hollow Ring, against something worse; and in the finale, the squad that chooses.

7. Life aboard (WD40)

WD40, what a day aboard is like:

  • Watches, not hours. Time aboard passes in watches: a mission, or a jump (TC67). After each, the crew comes off duty.
  • The after-jump meal. Every jump ends in the Galley, whoever cooked. It's where squads argue, rivalries soften and news from the last world is told.
  • Laps on the Track. Off duty, the Umas run the ring corridor around the observation dome. Nobody remembers who built it or why it's exactly that long. It's a seed of the last layer.
  • Shore leave. At every world, the crew goes down into the city in street clothes: the markets, a meal, a show, a keepsake for a cabin. It's where city exploration and life aboard meet.
  • Cabins as memory. Each Uma's cabin fills with things from the worlds she's been to: decor kits, keepsakes, cuttings from the Garden. Rooms are personal, and what she keeps says who she is (TC61).
  • The Guest Wing fills as heroines are freed. Some stay; some go home through their Gate when the ship can open it.
  • The bell. The Steward rings the watch with a sound none of the crew can place.

8. Bonds: the power of friendship and the power of love (WD41)

WD41, bonds are the one thing the Blackout can't eat.

  • The mechanism: the Blackout feeds on memory, but a memory two people share is held twice, and it can't be taken whole. Every bond scene is a memory made together, and some give back what was taken.
  • Friendship and love are the same power.
    • Trust and Heart give equal strength (TC56, TC58, SB15).
    • The story makes either one meaningful, and never makes love the "better" choice.
  • Resonance at rank 10: two memories holding each other, strong enough to stand against a Crowned in the finale (TC58).
  • Partners between Umas are the same thing between two Gatechildren: a shared childhood neither remembers, found again.
  • The tone (TC72, SB15):
    • everyone is an adult;
    • it's warm and teasing, never explicit, mutual, and never jealous;
    • every Uma is open to every captain, never exclusively;
    • she speaks first at rank 8;
    • a player can keep it to friendship.
  • In the plot: the Blackout's generals are those who lost everything they shared. The finale is won by a crew that remembers each other, with the captain holding what each of them forgot (WD24).

9. How the crew fights (WD42–WD45)

9.1 Frames (WD42)

WD42, Frames are the crew's cybernetic weapons: modular combat suits built around a core.

  • The parts are the concept's: arms, legs, core and back unit.
    • The forearm emitter fires the core's charge.
    • The projector throws a shield.
    • Shoulder and forearm mounts take swappable weapon and defence modules.
  • Helmets: a sealed helmet closes the Frame for vacuum, smoke and boarding.
  • Pilot suits: the crew's full-body ship suits link the wearer to the ship's posts, so a pilot suit at the helm is part of the ship.
  • The art matches: the approved looks show exactly these (Modular Frame 01, the shared Helmet 01, the ship-operating suit).
  • The cores are stolen siphon prototypes, the crew's original sin (WD3), given up in the finale.
  • Suit-up is the Frame snapping on, lit in her colour (TC39).
  • The five roles (Vanguard, Striker, Gunner, Netrunner, Medic) are the five ways a Frame is fitted (TC5).

9.2 On the ground (WD43)

WD43, ground battles are the grid tactics as built. They're fought in two kinds of place:

  • City maps: streets, markets, docks and rooftops, where investigation leads end;
  • Wild maps: reefs, canopy, calderas, platforms, ice and glass.

Each world's maps carry its environment's terrain: Tidewell's locks flood, Verdance's shrines hold elements, Anvil's lava vents, Gale's platforms tilt in the wind, Hush's ice cracks, Brink's glass cuts and reflects. Each environment's terrain is the game's to choose; the world only says what's there.

9.4 Boarding (WD45)

WD45, boarding is how fleets are freed.

  • Why board: a Collar is broadcast from a ship's command core, so breaking that core aboard frees every Collared heroine in the fleet at once. Ships also hold siphon cores worth seizing, prisoners worth rescuing, and contracts worth stealing.
  • How it plays: grid battles in the enemy's corridors, cargo holds and reactor rooms, helmets sealed, with the ship's own security as terrain: doors, turrets, vents and decompression.
  • The reverse: enemies board the Homestretch too (TC65), first at Anvil's slipways.
  • What the game decides: the board and the rules (request 2).
