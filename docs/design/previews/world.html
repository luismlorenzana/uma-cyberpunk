What's here and what's private WD22

Everything here is original and names no character: no Uma, no heroine, and no franchise's own terms beyond its title. Who plays which part (which Uma leads which route, which heroine leads which faction), the chapters and every line of dialogue are the story, kept in the ignored content/ (TC13).

The short answer

The Ring runs on stolen light WD1

The megacity glows because its Authority opens Gates into other worlds and siphons their power. The Gates aren't natural rifts, and the people they pull in aren't accidents.

The siphons let something in WD2

In the dark between worlds lives the Blackout, a hunger that eats and rewrites memory. Every siphon is a door for it, and the Authority's board has made a bargain with it.

The squad fights on the same sin WD3

The Umas' combat rigs run on prototype siphon cores, stolen from the Authority. They don't know that yet.

The heroines come to stop the theft WD9

Hired, bound, corrupted or simply lied to, they come through the Gates and the Authority points them at the squad. Each is held by one of three Holds, and each Hold has its own way to free her.

Eight factions WD10WD11

One per franchise, each with an original name, a reason to be on the Ring, a way to turn and one new battle mechanic.

Five acts WD15

From the Ring's underlevels out along the Lanes to a dead sister ring and back to the Gate Heart. Each act peels back one layer of the truth.

The last layer is the squad's own WD8

The Umas dream of running on a sunlit track under a sky they've never seen. They came through the first Gate as children, and the Blackout took the rest.

The tone is Star Guardian's WD20

Candy-bright neon over a tragic story, a found family, nobody dies on screen, and an Uma is never maimed.

1. The setting: the Ring, the Lanes and the Gates WD4WD5

A cross-section of one slice of the Ring. The city stacks in five layers over its hub, the Gate Heart. The Lanes run out to stations and moons, and to the dead sister ring. Eight Gate-worlds sit on either side, each a ring of light with a gap in its own colour, and every siphon runs back to the Gate Heart.

The Lanes and the Gates WD5

The Lanes

The routes between the Ring, its stations and its moons. Gates open along them and inside the Ring.

A ring of light with a gap

That's a Gate, each in its own colour. Every Gate-world has a glyph, and every faction from it carries that glyph.

Siphoning Gates hum

A Gate that's siphoning hums; one that's only a door is silent. That difference is the first clue (WD18).

Glyphs are ours

Each glyph is an original mark: the gap turned a different way around a simple shape. No franchise logos.

▼ The last layer

The Tithe WD1

The central truth, which both research passes reached on their own (references, "Where the references agree"). It makes the enemies right about something, so the factions are never just monsters.

The Blackout WD2

The darkness between worlds is a hunger, not an army. It feeds on fear and doubt, as Star Guardian's darkness does, and it eats and rewrites memory, which is 13 Sentinels' theme. A heroine it touches remembers the squad as her own world's enemy. One it takes completely is Dimmed: the game's fallen, her colours drained to one sickly accent.

The cores WD3

The power is a pact with a price, as in Star Guardian. Overdrive draws harder on the core and builds Strain (TC27). In the finale the squad gives the cores up.

The squad's own secret WD8

It ties the game to the Umas: they never race here, but they dream of it. It also makes the heroines their mirror. Everyone on the board was pulled through a Gate.

5.3 Who's behind whom WD13

The Blackout owns the Authority through its bargain. The Authority pays the Commission and the Contractors, commands the Muster and holds the Armada's treaty. The Blackout reaches the other four directly.

So every faction can be turned: by showing it the truth, or by burning the darkness out.

8. Tone, names and the ending WD20WD21WD24

Tone WD20

  • A found family.
  • Candy-bright neon over a tragic story, as Star Guardian does it.
  • No gore; nobody dies on screen.
  • An Uma is never maimed: a damaged pose is weary with the costume intact (uma-quest's rule).
  • A purged Crowned may go home through her Gate rather than join. That's the saddest the game gets.

Names WD21

Every name is ours. No term from 13 Sentinels, Star Guardian, Front Mission or Triangle Strategy, and no franchise's own term for a mechanic or a place. Where a franchise's mechanic inspires a faction's, ours has its own word:

staggervolleyRemnantoverloadFrost

The ending WD24

The squad shuts the siphon at the Gate Heart→ It gives up its cores→ The freed heroines lend their own power for the last battle, so the found family is literal→ The Ring dims but lives→ The Gates become doors that open both ways
