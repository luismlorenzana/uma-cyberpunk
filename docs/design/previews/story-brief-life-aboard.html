The story brief, part two: the captain, the squads, the factions and life aboard

Historical foundation. The four-act design and active roadmap govern future campaign work. Earlier calls and trials remain here as evidence.

Status: 2026-11-13 (D20). From the game session to the story session, after the owner's new direction for the game. This part is light and open on purpose: the owner asked for worldbuilding and base story elements first, "do not restrict that session". It adds to the story brief (SB1–SB11), which still holds where this doesn't change it. The game's side of the change is life-aboard.md (TC55–TC74); when your base elements land, the game session revises that design from them before writing its packets. Calls SB12–SB16, each a suggestion you can change. Visual page: previews/story-brief-life-aboard.html.

D28: the first base elements are in, as the world v2 (WD25–WD48): the plot, the eight worlds, the factions in depth, the ship, the captain, the squads, life aboard and the relationships. Next: the owner's read, then the private casting.

The short answer

  • The owner's new direction (SB12): the player is the captain of the ship, the point of view; the game leans toward squads, factions and one big overall plot more than individual stories; the captain can grow light dating-sim relationships with every character; the ship becomes a home and a large part of play, with cabins the Umas live in and duties they hold.
  • Start with worldbuilding (SB13): the big plot, the factions in depth, the ship and the captain, the squads, life aboard and the shape of the relationships. Write it as base elements (bibles), not scripts.
  • Nothing here is a requirement yet (SB14). The table of what the game will eventually read is for later, and every row has a default, so you can skip any of it.
  • A few things stay fixed (SB15), from the first brief and from the owner's new tone.
  • Deliver bibles first (SB16); the owner reads them, and then the game takes them as input.

1. The new direction (SB12)

In the owner's words (2026-11-13):

"a PoV character that would be the captain of the Ship supporting every individual Uma on their journeys, this game may not have that much of a focus on individual stories but instead on Squads and Factions and so on and a big overall plot, but I still want the PoV character to be able to develop some light dating sim relationship with all characters."

and: the ship as "a huge part of the gameplay"; "living quarters in the Ship that can be decorated", which the captain visits to build relationships, with "the 'power of friendship' and also the 'power of love'" as plot elements; and the Umas holding "responsibilities in the ship".

What it changes for the story:

Before (D4)Now (D20)
No point-of-view character; the squad and its leads carry the storyThe captain is the player's point of view and the one person every Uma talks to
The spine is the leads' chapters, Octopath Traveler's way, with a main thread betweenOne big plot through the factions and the ship's journey is the spine; a lead's Chapter 1 stays as her recruitment arc, as long or short as you like
Personal stories in each lead's later chaptersPersonal stories in bond scenes: short scenes with the captain, in her cabin or around the ship
The squad as one teamSquads: a few named teams aboard, with leaders, identities and rivalries
The factions as the enemies of each worldThe factions as powers with standing: they can be fought, bargained with, split, allied, turned

2. Where to start: base elements (SB13)

Prompts, not a checklist. Take them in any order, skip what doesn't spark, add what's missing:

  • The big plot. What's at stake across the worlds, and for whom? Who or what drives the conflict? How does it build across acts, and how could it end? Does the first pass's Blackout and Authority (world.md) survive, change or go?
  • The factions, in depth. For each of the eight (and any power you add): who they are in your world, what they want, how they live, who leads them and who dissents, what they think of each other and of the ship, what would move them from hostile to wary to allied, and what freeing one of their heroines means to them. The first pass's table (world.md, §5) is a starting point, not a frame.
  • The ship. Its name, where it came from, why it travels, whose it was, whether it has a voice of its own (an AI, a ghost in the reactor, the companion drones), and what its rooms mean: the Bridge, the Hangar, the Galley, the Observation Deck, the Garden, the Guest Wing for freed heroines.
  • The captain. Why this person commands this ship, what they lost or want, how the crew sees them at first and how that changes. Keep room for the player: the captain's face is never shown and their pronouns are the player's choice.
  • The squads. Which teams the crew forms, who leads them, what each stands for, how they rival or support each other; whether the rival squad (TC40) becomes one of them.
  • Life aboard. What a day on this ship is like: rituals, meals, music, shore leave, what each Uma does when she isn't fighting, what she keeps in her cabin.
  • The relationships. The flavour of the captain's bonds; which Umas have a story worth five bond scenes and which are lighter; friendships and rivalries between Umas (canonical partners); how the power of friendship and the power of love show up in the plot, not only in battle.

4. Still fixed (SB15)

From the first brief (SB3), unchanged: the crew is Umas only, by outfit; every enemy character is a heroine from the eight franchises, freed, never killed; the battle's rules are the game's; names are ours; casting and dialogue never go in git.

New with this part:

  • The captain is never drawn or described in the face, and their pronouns are the player's (she, he or they).
  • The dating layer is light: adults (every Uma in this game is drawn as an adult), warm and teasing, never explicit, mutual, no jealousy, and every Uma open to every captain, never exclusive.
  • Friendship and romance are equal in power. A Trust bond and a Heart bond give the same strength in battle, so the story can make either one meaningful without making romance the "better" choice.
  • She makes the first move. At rank 8 the Uma says what she hopes for before the captain chooses, so nobody is courted from above; a player can also set the game to Friends only.

5. How to deliver (SB16)

  • Bibles first, in Uma Story's layers (skeleton, then bible, then scripted): world, plot and factions as nameless docs in git (rewrite world.md as you see fit, or add pages beside it, each with a desktop page), and anything with names or casting in content/stories/bibles/ (private).
  • Tell the owner when a layer is ready. After the owner reads it, the game session revises life-aboard.md from it and writes Phase 4's and 5's packets.
  • Ask for what the game lacks with a story request in the handoff: a mechanic, a room, a kind of scene. The game answers in its next packet.
  • Start line for a new story session:
Read CLAUDE.md, docs/design/story-brief.md and docs/design/story-brief-life-aboard.md in ~/workspace/uma-cyberpunk, then take the story brief, starting with part two's base elements.
