Life aboard: the captain, the ship, the bonds and the duties

Status: 2026-11-13 (D20), for the owner's review. The owner set five directions to take the game past the sandbox (quoted in §1). Three research passes ran in parallel (research-life-aboard.md: ship building, crew duties, bonds and quarters), and this page designs the answer: a captain as the player's point of view, bonds with every Uma that feed the battles, quarters she lives in and the captain decorates and visits, duties aboard, and the ship as a game of its own: decks, modules, power and research. It also reshapes the story's spine toward squads, factions and one big plot. Its calls are TC55–TC74, new or amending the concept's (concept.md); every one is a recommendation, adopted until the owner changes it. The story session's open brief for it is story-brief-life-aboard.md. Visual page: previews/life-aboard.html.

The short answer

  • You are the Captain (TC55). The player names the captain and picks their pronouns; the captain is never drawn: the camera is the captain, as Blue Archive's Sensei is never seen. The captain commands from the Bridge and never stands on the grid. In battle the captain gives Orders, a few once-a-battle moves that grow with the ship and with the crew's trust.
  • Bonds with every Uma (TC56, TC57): ranks 1 to 10, raised by visits, gifts, talks, duties she enjoys and fighting under the captain's Orders. At rank 8 she says what she hopes for, and the player chooses Trust (friendship) or Heart (romance): open with everyone, never exclusive, never lost through neglect, and equal in power: the top of each path is the same strength with a different meaning, the power of friendship and the power of love.
  • Bonds fight (TC58, TC59). Each bond rank adds a small, reactive perk in battle, and rank 10 unlocks her Resonance: once a battle, on one Uma, the captain calls her name. Between Umas, partners who fight side by side build a pair bond (XCOM 2's soldier bonds) that ends in a duo technique.
  • Quarters (TC60, TC61). Every Uma gets a cabin on the Quarters deck, decorated slot by slot with furniture in six styles. Her Comfort helps her shake off Strain and makes the captain's visits count more. A visit is a short scene: talk, a gift, decorating together, or an invitation to one of the ship's spots.
  • The ship becomes a game (TC62–TC66). Decks of module slots on one screen, rooms with three levels, a Lab that researches the ship, the rigs and new Orders. Install everything, power a few: before each sortie the player powers the battle systems this map needs within the reactor's pips. The ship fights through ship support Orders (an orbital strike, a sweep, a barrier, a drone), Valkyria Chronicles 4's model, with no separate ship-battle mode; a finale's readiness shows which systems matter; later, boarding missions play on your own decks.
  • Duties (TC67, TC68). Each room has posts; an Uma's band in the post's stat sets how well she does it, by thresholds; her running style sets how she settles in. Posts produce once a watch, and a watch passes with each mission and each jump, never with real time. One ship-log card a stop, auto-assign in one tap, and nothing decays.
  • Squads and factions (TC69–TC71). The crew forms named squads that grow cohesion; squads not deployed run operations on the star map; each faction has a standing with the ship. The story's spine becomes one big plot told through the factions and the ship's journey; a lead's Chapter 1 stays as her recruitment arc.
  • The order (TC74): a new Phase 4, life aboard, on test content, while the story session writes the base elements; then the first worlds. Packets are written after the owner reviews this page.

What carries over unchanged: the battle and its rig (TC17–TC30), the crew as the Ranker's Umas by outfit (TC45), the eight factions and freeing heroines (TC26, TC37, TC38), Strain (TC27), the star map with no fuel and no clock (TC50), and everything Phases 1–3 built. 3. Bonds (TC56–TC59)

3.1 The captain and each Uma (TC56)

TC56, bonds. Every Uma aboard has a bond with the captain, from rank 1 to 10, shown as a ten-segment ring on her profile and her cabin door.

RankUnlocksGate
1She's aboard: her cabin, her profile, small talk—
2Her first bond scene—
3Her perk I (TC58)—
4Her second bond scene; she can be invited out (§4.3)The story's first act done
5Her perk II—
6Her third bond sceneThe story's second act
7Her perk III—
8Her fourth bond scene, where she signals what she hopes for, and the player chooses Trust or Heart (below); her perk IV, EndureThe story's third act
9Her perk V—
10Her last bond scene, her Resonance (TC58) and her epilogue cardThe story's last act
  • Rapport fills each rank: a visit's talk (more when the topic is one she likes), a gift (more for a favourite), a duty she enjoys (a little a watch), a battle she fought in (a little, more when she was covered, joined a Crossfire or an Order helped her), a reply to her message after a battle (one tap), and choices in her bond scenes. The numbers are tuned so a favourite reaches rank 10 by the end of the story with modest attention.
  • Catch-up: an Uma below the crew's median rank gains rapport twice as fast, so a late recruit is never a chore.
  • Gates tie the upper ranks to the story's acts (Fire Emblem's support caps, Persona's story locks), so nothing can be rushed and every rank lands at a moment the story can use.
  • Never lost, never missed: rapport never decays, a missed visit costs nothing, no Uma is jealous of another, and a rank's scene waits until the player wants it (its rank's reward isn't held behind it) and replays from the Log.
  • After a battle, she messages (Blue Archive's MomoTalk, Star Rail's phone): a line about the fight, and a one-tap reply for a little rapport. Cheap contact for a crew of a hundred.

The two paths at rank 8 (Persona's rank-9 choice, Fire Emblem's platonic S-supports, Dragon Age II's equal rewards), without their pressure:

  • She makes the first move. Her rank-8 scene is hers: she says what she hopes for, and only then does the player choose. It answers the captain's power over the crew: nobody is courted from above.
  • A global setting, Romance: Ask or Friends only, set at a new game and changeable aboard. With Friends only, every rank-8 scene is written toward Trust.

At the end: every Uma at rank 10 gets her own epilogue card (Fire Emblem's paired endings). An optional last question, "who stands beside you", may name one of them or nobody, and locks nothing before it.

Tone (TC72): adults, as every Uma in this game is (the art category is the Combat Adult one); warm, teasing, sincere, never explicit: confessions, dates, hands held, a kiss implied or faded out; no touch minigames, no bath or peeping gags (Sakura Wars' and Fire Emblem Fates' most criticized scenes); mutual and consent-forward; no gifts-for-affection grind, no jealousy, and an Uma's worth in battle never depends on romancing her.

3.2 Bond scenes (TC57)

TC57, bond scenes are where personal stories live now. Since the main line is the plot and the factions (TC71), each Uma's personal story moves into her bond scenes: short scenes (one to two minutes) in her cabin or a ship spot, at ranks 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10.

UmaBond scenesWritten by
A lead (recruited through her own Chapter 1)All five, in her voiceThe story session
A crew Uma the story featuresThree (ranks 2, 8, 10), the rest small talkThe story session
Any other crew UmaSmall talk from her tastes and tags; a scene set the story session can add laterThe game, from tags (§4.4)

Between scenes she still talks: systemic lines drawn from her cabin, her Strain, the last battle, her post and her last gift, so a hundred Umas feel alive without five hundred written scenes. A personal operation (Mass Effect 2's loyalty missions) can gate a lead's rank 4: an operation (TC70) or a mission about her, if the story writes one.

Bond scenes use the scene player as it is (TC36) plus two new steps: a captain's choice (two or three tones, with rapport) and the path choice at rank 8. They replay from the Log.

3.3 Bonds in battle (TC58)

TC58, every rank shows up on the grid, in small readable steps. The perks are the same for every Uma, so no one has to be romanced or courted to be useful:

RankPerkIn the rules
3Ready: she starts each battle with +1 ChargeHer Charge at the start
5Answer: when an ally next to her suffers a Rig Break, she gains 1 ChargeA cue on Rig Break
7Sure: her chance skills +5%The skill chance
8Endure: once a battle, a hit that would break her Core leaves it at 1 (Persona's Endure; Guts' Hold on for anyone)Rig Break
9Steadfast: her Cover costs no Charge once a battleCover's cost
10Resonance: Resolve (Trust) or Devotion (Heart), belowA captain's Order on her

Every perk is small, reactive or once a battle (Persona's Follow Up and Endure, Azur Lane's and Blue Archive's small stat steps): her kit is whole without any of them, and no bond is required to win.

Resonance (the power of friendship and the power of love): the captain's Order Call her name, once a battle and on one Uma only, however many are at rank 10 (so a squad of rank-10s doesn't stack). She takes her next turn at once with full Charge, and her attack that turn draws a Crossfire from every Linked ally in reach (XCOM 2's Dual Strike, Chrono Trigger's techs). It plays a short cut-in in her colour: Resolve reads as "I've got you", Devotion as "for you". Same numbers, different meaning.

Between Umas: partners (TC58 too; XCOM 2: War of the Chosen's soldier bonds, Chrono Trigger's dual techs):

  • Two Umas who fight in the same battle, or share a duty post, build camaraderie, faster after a shared moment (one covers the other from a Rig Break, a Medic reboots her). At a threshold the player can make them partners: one partner per Uma at a time, so the pairs stay readable. The bonds come out of play, not scripts.
  • Partner level 1: when one attacks, the other joins as Crossfire without spending Charge, once a battle. Level 2: each covers the other for free, once a battle. Level 3: a duo technique, a combined attack both spend a turn on, its look from their two roles.
  • The story session can name canonical pairs (old friends, rivals, sisters); the player can pair anyone.

4. Quarters (TC60, TC61)

4.1 Cabins (TC60)

TC60, every Uma has a cabin. The Quarters deck holds cabins: shared berths early, private cabins as the ship's Quarters modules grow (TC62), so room is a reason to build. A cabin is a small diorama, landscape, with six decor slots and a window:

SlotTakes
BedBeds and bunks
DeskDesks, consoles, vanities
WallPosters, screens, shelves
FloorRugs, plants, a training mat
LightLamps, neon signs, string lights
KeepsakeOne unique item: a trophy, a photo, a gift from the captain, something from her world
WindowShows the world the ship orbits (not a slot)

Slots, not free placement: quick on a phone (Blue Archive's café and Azur Lane's dorm show free placement is a chore at this size), and every item can be drawn once at one size.

4.2 Decor and Comfort (TC61)

TC61, Comfort. Every item has a style and a comfort of 1 to 3. Six styles: Neon, Classic, Cozy, Sporty, Tech, Wild. Each Uma has a favourite style, a style she dislikes, a favourite gift kind and two topics she loves talking about (tags, §4.4).

  • Comfort = each item's comfort, +2 for each item in her favourite style, −1 for each in the one she dislikes, and +2 for a full set (six items of one line), shown as five lights on her cabin door. Six slots means only her best items count: clutter never pays (Genshin's flaw).
  • Her tastes are discovered, not listed: her first gift, her first decorating choice and her talks reveal her favourite and disliked styles on her profile. A few Umas are contrarians, as Spiritfarer's Elena hates improvements.
  • Comfort 3 or more: when she rests through a mission, she sheds 1 Strain even below 3 (TC27 otherwise clears it only when she sits out at 3). Comfort 5: resting clears all her Strain.
  • A visit in a cabin at Comfort 4 or 5 earns half again as much rapport.
  • Shared spaces (the Galley, the Observation Deck) give the whole crew a small rest bonus; her cabin adds hers.
  • Never required: one-tap theme kits furnish a cabin in her favourite style from the stock, so a hundred cabins are never a chore, and no bond rank ever waits on decorating.
  • Requests: now and then a visit ends with her asking for something (Spiritfarer's requests): a plant, a lamp, a poster from a world; granting it pays rapport and fills a slot.
  • Items come from the Store, the worlds' markets, salvage, the Workshop (TC62), the Garden's plants, and bond scenes (keepsakes). They're ours, so Claude draws them in code as uma-art pixel items (uma-platform P126, P147); no character art is needed.

4.3 A visit (TC61)

Aboard, the captain can visit three Umas each time the ship returns from a mission or arrives at a world, about two minutes in all:

ChoiceWhat happens
TalkThree topics; one she loves gives full rapport and any other half (NIKKE's advise, with no fail state); a few lines in her voice (her tags, or her written small talk)
GiftOne item a visit; her favourite kind gives the most
DecorateShe picks an item from the ship's stock for a slot, and says why; Comfort changes on the spot
Invite (rank 4+)An outing to a spot: the Observation Deck, the Galley, the Garden, the Sim Pod, or a world's market on shore leave; a short scene with a bonus rapport
Her bond sceneWhen one is ready, the visit opens with it

She's in her cabin when she's off duty and not deployed; on duty she's at her post, and a visit there is a shorter talk. A badge on her door says when she has something: a bond scene ready, a request, a message. A visit ends on a result card: rapport gained, Strain shed, a request if she has one. About sixty to ninety seconds each.

4.4 Tastes and tags (TC61)

Each Uma needs: a favourite style, a disliked style, a favourite gift kind, two loved topics, a liked duty and, if the story gives her one, a partner. The story session tags them for the Umas it features (private, in the casting); the game derives the rest from what it knows: her running style, her role and her stats set defaults, so all 100 work from day one and the story only has to write the ones it cares about.

5. The ship (TC62–TC66)

5.1 Decks and modules (TC62)

TC62, the ship is built. The cutaway (CY10) becomes a hull of decks, each a row of module slots.

  • One screen: the ship is a side cross-section of its decks, XCOM 2's Avenger and Suikoden IV's decks, where a tap opens a room. No walking hub (Three Houses' and Engage's most cited chore).
  • The ship starts with two decks of four slots. A new deck comes at a story refit (a shipyard world) or by research, up to four decks of six. Rooms open as leads come aboard (TC49, Suikoden's castle) and are fitted with credits, Alloy (from salvage and dismantled parts) and Data, over one to three watches (TC67).
  • A module is a room, one or two slots wide, with levels 1 to 3; each level visibly changes its art. Each room has duty posts (§6) and does more at each level. No upkeep: nothing costs credits to keep (BattleTech's and Starsector's bills would quietly bring back the clock TC50 rejected).
  • Power: install everything, power a few (FTL, XCOM 2, Starfield, Valkyria Chronicles 4). The rooms aboard (cabins, the Galley, the Lab…) simply run. Engineering's reactor has a few pips, and the ship's battle systems (the Battery, Sensors, the Shield Projector, the Drone Bay, the Engines) cost one to three pips each. Before each sortie the player powers the systems this battle needs within the reactor's pips: sensors for a fogged map, the shield for a siege. The reactor grows by research. One number, chosen per battle.
  • No trap modules: every room and system has a situation where it's the best pick (XCOM 2's Workshop and early Laboratory are the warning).

5.3 The ship in battle (TC64)

TC64, the ship supports the squad without a second battle mode. Ship battles stay deferred (TC16): a real-time or second tactical mode would double the work and split the game. Valkyria Chronicles 4's Ship Orders (its land cruiser's radar, cannon fire, repair and rescue calls, capped per battle and grown by research) are the closest model, and XCOM 2's Avenger the second:

  • Ship support Orders (TC59) from the powered battle systems (§5.1): the orbital strike, the sweep, the barrier, the drone; once a battle each, stronger at the system's level and with a matching Uma on its post. The Hangar adds a reinforcement Order at level 3: a resting Uma drops in. The bench's support call (TC27) stays separate.
  • Duty boosts: a skilled pilot, a cook's meal, a medic in the Med Bay each show up as a small, named effect at the battle's start, listed on the deploy screen.
  • Readiness (Mass Effect 2's finale, without its deaths): before a big battle, the star map shows which systems and rooms matter, and each one missing adds one harsh condition to the map (fog without Sensors, a hull breach hazard without the Shield Projector, no reinforcements without the Hangar's level 3). Shown in advance; never permadeath.

5.4 Boarding missions (TC65)

TC65, later: missions on your own ship. Some story or operation missions are boardings: the enemy comes aboard, and the battle map is built from the player's own deck layout, each room a section with its own cover and props. Defending the ship you built is the payoff of building it. A Phase 6 addition, once the maps can be generated.

5.5 Travel (TC66)

TC66, a jump is a watch, and may bring an event. Travel stays a tap with no fuel and no clock (TC50).

  • Each jump passes a watch (TC67) and may draw one event card (FTL's beacons, Sunless Skies' storylets): a one-tap choice resolved by who's on duty (a good pilot avoids the ion storm), paying Alloy, Data, a Lead, an item or rapport. Never a forced fight; skippable; written by the story session as pools per region if it wants them, with a small generic pool from the game.
  • The ship opens the map (Final Fantasy's airships, Skies of Arcadia): a region of space can need a drive tier, the storm shielding or the sensors to reach or reveal (derelicts, salvage caches, a hidden world). Capability, not fuel.
  • The hull shows its growth on the star map: its sprite gains decks and systems as they're built.

The battle systems' posts (Navigator on Sensors, Engineer on the Shield Projector, Mechanic on the Drone Bay, Pilot on the Engines) are the same people as the rooms' posts: an Uma holds one post at a time.

Aptitude, by thresholds, not sums (Fallout Shelter's legible stat-keyed rooms, Star Traders' capped pools, so no spreadsheets): in the post's stat, band 3 does the job, band 4 or 5 adds its extra effect, band 2 or less works at half. A matching role adds one named perk, not a multiplier. Her liked duty adds a little rapport each watch. The result shows before she's assigned.

Running style sets how she settles in, the Uma's own trait put to work:

Station chiefs (Skies of Arcadia's crew perks): an Uma the story tags as a room's chief brings a unique perk to its post. Optional story data; without it, nobody is a chief.

6.2 What it asks of the player (TC68)

TC68, duties never become chores (Arknights' and the gacha bases' lesson, inverted for a single-player game; every automation those games added after launch is here from the start):

  • Duties persist until changed. Auto-assign fills every post with the best fits among those not deployed, and suggests Umas resting at Strain 3 for the Med Bay and the Observation Deck; presets keep two arrangements.
  • One ship-log card on arrival lists what every post made; it's applied at once. Nothing to collect, nothing expires, no notifications. The target: under fifteen seconds a stop, and most stops need no input.
  • Nothing decays: an empty post produces nothing, and that's all. No morale meter on duty: Strain (TC27) stays the one fatigue, and duties don't add to it. An Uma resting at Strain 3 can still stand duty, so the bench has a purpose.
  • A soft cost to deploying: an Uma deployed in a battle works her post at half for that watch (a Pace Chaser at full). Between no decision at all (Genshin's expeditions) and a hard lock that steals the squad (Girls' Frontline's logistics).
  • No real time, no timers, no daily logins: watches advance only as the player plays.
  • No failure rolls on duty, ever: output is deterministic.
  • Duty partners: two Umas on the same room's posts build camaraderie (TC58), so friendships form off the grid too.

7. Squads, operations and factions (TC69–TC71)

7.1 Squads (TC69)

TC69, the crew forms squads. Up to four named squads of four (plus the guest slot), each with a leader, a name and a banner colour; an Uma belongs to one at a time, or none. The player can still build an ad hoc squad.

  • Cohesion rises each battle a squad fights together, to level 5: +1 Charge for the leader at level 1, Crossfire reach +1 at 3, one free Cover each at 5 (tuned by the simulator).
  • The story session names the squads it wants canonical (a flagship squad, a faction-born squad of defectors, the rival squad if it joins, TC40); the player names the rest.

7.2 Operations (TC70)

TC70, squads not deployed run operations. On the star map, Comms lists operations: secondary missions on worlds already visited, each with a danger level, a length of one to three watches and a reward (credits, Alloy, Data, items, faction standing, sometimes a crew Uma or a Lead).

  • Send a squad; it resolves on its return with a short report: success by the squad's power against the danger, with its cohesion and its partners helping.
  • Never catastrophic: a failed operation pays less and adds Strain; nobody is lost.
  • This is where "squads and factions" plays out at scale (Mass Effect's war assets, XCOM's covert operations), without more battles to play by hand.

7.3 Faction standing (TC71)

TC71, factions, and the story's spine.

  • Each faction has a standing with the ship, from hostile to allied, moved by the story, by operations and by which heroines are freed. Standing opens operations, Store stock and guests.
  • Readiness (Mass Effect 3's war assets, as one visible meter): faction standing, operations won and the ship's systems add up to the fleet's readiness for the finale, shown on the Bridge, so everything the crew did on the way arrives at the end.
  • The story's spine is one big plot told through the factions and the ship's journey. This amends TC8 and TC51: the leads' Chapter 1s stay (Phase 3 built them) as recruitment arcs, shorter if the story wants; later personal chapters give way to bond scenes (TC57) and squad missions; the main line is the plot's. How the factions move, ally, betray and turn is the story session's (the open brief).

8. The session, the save and the art (TC72, TC73)

TC72 (amends TC36), the session: a scene of three minutes at most; a battle of about ten; aboard up to five: duties (auto-assign, half a minute), a build or a research pick, up to three visits, an operation sent. All of it optional between battles.

TC72 also sets the tone of the dating layer: adults, warm and never explicit, mutual, no jealousy, no punishment for not romancing, Heart open to every captain with every Uma, and the same battle power for Trust and Heart (§3.1).

TC73, the save and the art:

  • The save grows in steps, one version a packet as each part lands: the captain (name, pronouns); bonds (rank, rapport, path, scenes seen); partners and camaraderie; cabins and the item stock; the ship (decks, modules, levels, Power, the project in the Lab); posts; the watch count; squads and cohesion; operations under way; faction standing. Every step keeps the production-save rules.
  • Art, by kind (uma-platform P126, P147): furniture, ship modules, the cabin dioramas and room marks are ours, so Claude draws them in code (pixel art and kit art in a uma-platform session, published as uma-art). The Umas use the busts, faces and chibis they already have. The captain is never drawn (TC55), so no new character art is needed.
