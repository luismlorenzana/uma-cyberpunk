"the full app buildout and some of the combat testing side of things", and to "see what we can reuse from the uma-platform HD-2D engine and so on".

This page answers both: what's reused and how, the shape of the code, how the combat is tested, the app's screens, and the packets in order. The short answer

Reuse first, in three ways BP2

Import the platform packages (uma-kit's sign-in, update prompt, shell and roster tiles; uma-data's master data; uma-server's guard and proxies; uma-stage's parts; uma-art's atlases); copy what each sibling game copies (uma-quest's wiring, save sync and PWA set-up; uma-farm's story delivery; uma-rpg's place registry and star chart); and follow patterns where the code can't be shared (uma-rpg's battle reducer and autosave-by-replay, uma-racer's simulator).

The HD-2D engine fits as parts, not as a finished stage BP3

uma-stage 0.3.0's two scenes are uma-quest's field and uma-farm's band, and neither suits a grid. Its terrain builder, figures, picking, camera, fade, effects and frame pacer do, so the board is a game-owned scene, as uma-quest's fights already are. Four stage requests are likely (tunable bloom, glowing frames, a tactics kit kind, a saver tier), filed one at a time by CY7.

No sibling has grid battle code BP4

So the rules are new: a pure, seeded package, @cyber/battle, on uma-rpg's reducer pattern.

The combat is tested three ways BP5

Before any story depends on it: unit tests and golden replays, a sandbox on the phone, and a headless simulator.

The whole app is built on test content BP6BP7

The ship and its rooms, the Bridge's star map, a world's map, the mission flow with resume, the crew, the Hangar and the story runner. The story session's files then drop in.

Two tracks after CY1 BP8

The foundation (the cloud and the hello app) and the combat (the rules, the AI and simulator, the board). They meet at the sandbox, CY8, and the app follows in CY9–CY12.

Where master data stops

master data's stats→ @cyber/domain's one adapter→ plain numbers: bands, part HP, weapon stats→ @cyber/battle, which depends on nothing

@cyber/battle depends on nothing

It takes plain numbers (bands, part HP, weapon stats), so the simulator and its tests run anywhere, and master data's shape never leaks into the rules. @cyber/domain holds the adapter (uma-platform's architecture §2.4: one adapter module per game).

ESM for the web, CommonJS for the API

The web reads the packages' ESM source; the API reads a CommonJS build, as in uma-quest. Heavy code (the board, three.js, the story runner) loads lazily, and the main bundle stays under 180 KB gzip (check-size.mjs and its LAZY_MODULES).

uma-quest's field and uma-farm's band; their scenes are private and their cameras don't fit a grid.

A Phaser top-down town on a 16 px grid. The ship is rooms, not a walkable town.

Not reused uma-work

Timed jobs; installed (D2) but unused until a room needs a timer.

Not reused Any sibling's battle rules

They're round-based with no grid, facing or height.

3. The HD-2D board on uma-stage BP3

The scene CY7 builds, in apps/web/src/board/. The battle state sends cues; a game-owned three.js scene draws them from uma-stage's parts; the DOM sits over the canvas.

The rules: @cyber/battle, decides everything Game-owned scene Imported from uma-stage DOM

What fits on a phone

One texel per device pixel makes a tile 128 px wide and about 73 px deep. On the owner's 3× iPhone in landscape that's about 20 tiles across and 16 deep: a flat 14 × 14 board fits, and heights at the back overflow, so the board pans. A 2× phone fits about 10 tiles each way and would need a zoom (a request, only if needed).

A 14 × 14 board on screen Off screen: the board pans, or needs a zoom One tile: 128 × about 73 device pixels, at 35°

What CY7 checks before anything builds on it

1

A map built directly into roomGeometry's design object, with a level-3 tile: if it builds, heights 0–3 need no request.

2

Picking on every tile top at about 24 pt deep, with heights.

3

Thirteen figures live (four sprite sets and nine standees, about 56 MB of the 112 MB budget).

4

Frame rate with the saver on and off (FpsRecorder: mean, 1% low), against 60 fps with a 30 fps floor.

5

How much heights hide, with the fade on.

4. The battle package BP4

A battle is a frozen input plus a list of actions. The save keeps exactly that, so an autosave after every action is a few bytes, and a resume replays to the same state.

packages/battle/src/

types.tsMap, Tile, Unit, Rig, Part, Loadout, Weapon, Action, Cue, Objective
tuning.tsevery number in one table: bands, part HP, Capacity, damage, hit, Charge (TC4, TC17–TC20)
rng.tsa seeded hash: (seed, turn, actor, roll) → number; never Math.random
battle.tscreateBattle(input) → state; applyBattleAction(state, action) → { state, cues, error }

sight.tsline of fire and the pincer test for Crossfire

5. Combat testing, three ways BP5

WayWhat it isRun byCatches
1. Tests and golden replaysVitest per rule; golden files hold an input, its actions and the exact cuespnpm test, in CIA rule that changed by accident
2. The sandboxA route in the app: pick a map, squad, loadouts, enemies and seed; play with the full battle UI; save the setup as a scenarioThe owner, on the phone; Claude headless through ?debugHow it feels: readability, taps, the ten-minute target
3. The simulatorpnpm sim plays scenarios AI against AI over many seeds and writes a reportClaude, after any tuning changeBalance: win rates, battle length, what dominates
Scenarios are small JSON files in scenarios/, shared by the sandbox and the simulator, nameless (stand-in units, test maps, generic enemies), so they belong in git.

The simulator's report

pnpm sim all --seeds 500, written to the ignored sim/out/:

MeasureTarget
Win rate in an even scenario45–55%
Battle length8–14 player turns, about ten minutes at the sandbox's measured pace
A full-HP Core broken in one hit, at equal bandsNever, except a boss's signature
Each weapon band's share of damageNo band above 40% in a mixed squad
Counters, Crossfires and Covers a battleAt least one each, on average
Parts broken before the CoreMore than half the Rig Breaks
Charge left unspent at the endLow, or Charge is too cheap

A balance pass

edit tuning.ts→ rerun pnpm sim all --seeds 500→ the report in sim/out/→ the report's diff goes in the packet's decision
