2. The owner's direction SB2

"interplanetary type world, where maybe the whole system you described is only Chapter 1 for every Uma and then after finishing that they join into 'the spaceship' which would act as the hub or stronghold, then, when traveling to other worlds there would be other Umas to pickup on their own Chapter 1, a little like the Octopath Traveler approach"

In the owner's words (D4)

What the game builds from it

Interplanetary

Worlds are separate settings, reached by the ship.

The first world is D3's Ring

The neon city on an orbital ring, with its layers and its Gates (WD4, WD5). The owner calls that whole system Chapter 1.

Every lead Uma has a Chapter 1 on her own world

Finishing it brings her aboard.

The ship is the hub and stronghold

The rooms between battles, the Bridge's star map and the crew's home.

The ship travels and picks up leads

Each on her own Chapter 1, as Octopath's travelers are met in their home towns.

The shape, Octopath's way

Each world is a node; on each, a lead's Chapter 1 ends with her coming aboard the ship, which then travels on to the next world. One ship, drawn at each pickup: it grows a room with every lead.

a lead Uma on her own world her Chapter 1; finishing it brings her aboard the ship travels on to the next world a room she opens or upgrades aboard (TC49)

Later chapters, crossed paths and the main thread run from the ship between pickups (§4, lesson 4 in §5). The counts are the game's defaults (SB7), yours to change.

3. What's fixed SB3

These come from the owner, the platform or the battle. Changing one is the owner's call or a story request (SB10).

1

The crew is Umas only

The Ranker's 100 Playable Umas, keyed by outfit id (a sister shares her base Uma's character id), and the Uma NPCs board for rivals and Uma NPCs. They never race; they can remember or dream of it. Minor original characters (a ship's AI, a broker, a world's officials) are fine; named heroes are Umas.

2

Every enemy character is a heroine

From the platform's Boss, Key and Regular boards for the eight franchises (Azur Lane, Genshin Impact, Wuthering Waves, Honkai: Star Rail, Zenless Zone Zero, Honkai Impact 3rd, Punishing: Gray Raven and NIKKE). They fight as eight factions with original names. Each faction also fields original machines. The franchises' own monsters stay out.

3

A heroine is freed, never killed

Her Hold decides how: an Anchor (a corruption grafted on her rig, broken in battle), a Collar (someone else's control, broken in battle), or None (deceived or hired: win, and the story brings her over). A Netrunner can also Jack-Out an Anchor or a Collar. Each heroine's Hold is yours to pick, privately. A new kind of Hold is a request.

Anchor · broken in battleCollar · broken in battleNone · win, the story brings her overJack-Out · Anchor or Collar
4

No one dies in battle

A broken Core is a Rig Break: she's out of the fight, weary, costume intact. An Uma is never maimed. Whether anyone dies off the battlefield is yours; D3's tone rule was no one on screen.

5

The shape of play

A scene of three minutes at most; a battle of about ten; a chapter of two to four battles; a new game's first battle within two minutes; a session of about fifteen minutes.

≤ 3min a scene≈ 10min a battle 2–4battles a chapter≤ 2min to the first battle ≈ 15min a session
6

Each faction brings one battle mechanic, and the game builds it

(world.md WD11: cover and volley, stagger, reactions, artillery, typed shields and time, Remnants and overload, infection, phases.) You decide where and why a faction appears and may rename it, but at most two new mechanics in one world, so the battle teaches one idea at a time.

7

Names are ours

No term from the four reference works, and no franchise's own term for a person, place or mechanic. The franchises' own worlds stay off the star map: the worlds are ours, and the factions come to them. Visiting a franchise's world would mean retelling it, in its names.

8

Privacy

Docs in git name no character: no Uma, no heroine, nothing from a franchise beyond its title. Who plays which part, the chapters and every line live in the ignored content/ (CLAUDE.md; scripts/check-private-content.test.mjs runs in CI). Check git status before every commit.

5. What Octopath Traveler teaches SB5

The owner named Octopath as the model. A research pass on both games and their reception (sources below) gives the facts and seven lessons. The game already builds for each lesson (TC51); the story decides how to use it.

How Octopath works

Octopath Traveler (2018)Octopath Traveler II (2023)
The startPick one of eight travelers; she stays in the party until her last chapterThe same
RecruitingVisit her home town; play her Chapter 1 now, or later at a tavernThe same; a skipped recruit joins low and as a stranger
ChaptersFour each, 32 in allThree to five each, some with branches
GatingEach chapter's flag on the map shows a level, about ten levels apart; each area shows a danger levelThe same
PartyFour, swapped at taverns; the bench earns nothing, blamed for grindingThe same
Path actionsOne per traveler, in noble and rogue pairs (Inquire or Scrutinize, Purchase or Steal…)A day action and a night action each
Connecting the storiesOptional two-person banter skits after story scenes; chapter cutscenes show only the leadCrossed Paths: four fixed pairs, two parts each; banter replayable
The finaleHidden behind obscure side stories; all eight fight in two teams of fourSignposted: unlocks after all stories and Crossed Paths; the whole cast in its scenes
TravelOn foot, fast travel to visited townsFerries, and a ship bought late for the open sea

What players and critics said

The first game's stories were "strictly siloed", every chapter ran the same shape (town, path action, dungeon, boss), nothing explained why the eight travelled together, and the true ending needed a guide. Its defenders liked the focus on one hero at a time. The sequel's director said Crossed Paths came straight from players asking for "dialogue between protagonists beyond travel banter", and that each story was rebuilt to feel like a different RPG. Reviews called the signposted finale a big improvement, but still wanted more connection: each traveler pairs only once, and other characters ignore the party in cutscenes.

Seven lessons for this game

1

The ship is the reason to travel together,

which Octopath's first game lacked. Give each lead a part to play aboard (the room she opens, TC49).

2

Write the crew into every scene, cheaply:

one to three short interjection slots per scene, each filled by a lead who's in the squad, with a fallback line; and a short debrief aboard after each chapter, replayable from the Log.

3

Pair every lead at least twice

in crossed paths, the first part short and early.

4

Break up the run of Chapter 1s.

Keep each short (one or two battles). Put a later chapter or a crossed path between pickups. A new lead joins at the squad's level, never level 1.

5

Give each lead's chapters their own shape:

three to five chapters, a branch here and there, and objectives other than beating a boss (escape, defend, escort, a heist).

6

Show the gating:

a danger level on every chapter, in small steps. The bench earns half XP (TC27), so a lead who sat out isn't stranded.

7

Signpost the finale from the start:

seed the shared threat in every lead's chapters, list its conditions, and bring the whole crew into it (two squads in turn).

Sources: Kotaku on the structure · Famitsu interview, via Nintendo Everything · Push Square interview · Siliconera on travel banter · RPG Site review · Shacknews review of II · Engadget review of II · RPG Site on Crossed Paths · Game8 story list · Gameranx on the ship. The first game's chapter levels come from player reports, not an official table.

8. The content contract SB8

The game reads the story from files, the way uma-quest and uma-farm do: the format and its validator live in git, the content never does.

Live since CY9 (D13). The parser and the check are built: run pnpm story:check in this repo to validate content/stories/ (it writes checks.json beside your files and exits non-zero on any error). The exact format, every step's syntax and every rule are in the manual's story page. Settled beyond the sketch below: wait is ms; flag is a slug or { flag, set }; file is { file, line }; anomaly is { anomaly, open }; join and aboard are an outfit id; call and battle a slug; a file's id is its name; locations carry danger, needs and links; casting.json holds leads, crewOrder, heroines and minor; any file may carry a layer; a mission may name the game's scenario.
Since CY13 (D22): bond scenes are live: bonds/<outfit>.json (scenes at ranks 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10, and an epilogue), the captain step (two or three answers in a tone, with rapport), the path step (Trust or Heart, at rank 8), bondGates in the casting, and {captain} and the pronoun placeholders in any line (the captain and bonds page).

Ids

An Uma is her outfit id from master data (a sister shares her base Uma's character id, so never the character id alone). A heroine is her board id from the Ranker. Everything ours (worlds, places, chapters, scenes, missions, Files, Calls, minor characters) has a short lowercase slug of your choosing that never changes once used. Names, portraits and busts come from master data at run time, so the files never repeat them.

Umaoutfit idHeroineboard idOursa lowercase slug, fixed once used

The files carry ids only; every name and picture is looked up at run time.

10. How the two sessions work together SB10

One repo

Both sessions work in uma-cyberpunk and commit to main in Conventional Commits style (fetch first): the story's commits are docs(story): ….

Decisions

Go in decisions.md with the next free D-number, from either session.

A story request

A new mechanic, hook, room, talent or Hold is a row in the handoff's "Story requests" table (handoff). The game session answers it in its next packet or says why not.

This brief

Is the game session's. When the game changes a hook, it updates the brief and logs a D-decision.

In docs/design/previews/, as the owner asks of every doc.

To build maps and test chapters, and never commits it.
