Where the references agree

The strongest findings are the ones two passes reached on their own.

The central truth WD1

13 Sentinels: “the Drain”+Star Guardian: “the Tithe”=one idea

The 13 Sentinels pass proposed “the Drain” (the Ring's power comes from Gates that bleed other worlds) and the Star Guardian pass proposed “the Tithe” (the Ring's Authority siphons other worlds' suns). They're the same idea, from a mystery angle and a cosmology angle, so the world is built on it.

One resource for the turn TC20

Triangle Strategy: TP+Front Mission: spend-or-keep AP=Charge

They collapse into one small bar, Charge : skills spend it, counters and links need it, waiting banks it.

The attack cut-in TC12TC28

Front Mission: side view on every attack+adult combat art, side-on=the cut-in

Front Mission cuts to a side view on every attack, and the platform's adult combat art is drawn side-on, facing one way. So the cut-in is where the adult art lives, and the map uses the four-facing chibi sets.

No death TC25TC26

the pilot walks away+guardians are redeemed+uma-quest never maims=Rig Break · freeing

Front Mission's pilot walks away from a wrecked body; Star Guardian's guardians fall and are redeemed; uma-quest never maims an Uma. Defeat is a Rig Break for the squad and a freeing for a heroine.

13 Sentinels: Aegis Rim

Story structure
Vanillaware and AtlusJapan 2019Worldwide 2020Switch 2022 Metacritic 85–88Over a million sold
What it does

The six route lenses: heist, noir, idol scandal, street-racing crew, ghost-in-the-net and smuggling run. Each ends on a shared beat.

Three modes that gate each other

  • The adventure: one route per protagonist, in episodes of 20–30 minutes, played in any order. A route can lock until another character's route reaches a scene, a battle is won, an archive entry opens, or overall progress passes a mark. Each route has a flowchart with hints for its locks.
  • The battle mode: real-time-with-pause defence of a terminal against waves. Pilots on the bench guard the terminal; a fatigue meter benches pilots who fight too often.
  • The archive: wiki-style entries, some opened by the story and some bought with points from top battle ranks; plus a timeline of every scene, browsable by place, loop and character, and replayable.

It opens at the end

  • The tutorial is the final battle, so the mystery is how, not whether.

Keywords

  • Words picked up in talk and items can be thought about or used on people.
  • Used in another route, they open branches.

Cast balance

  • Each protagonist gets a self-contained route that ends on the same beat, and appears as support in the others.

How the twists are planted

  • Contradictions between routes are deliberate clues.
  • A character who looks like a villain in one route gets context in her own.
  • Small oddities pile up (a different surname in a note, shared dreams of wars never fought).
  • Each route reads as a familiar sci-fi genre story, so players file every oddity under genre convention.
What players said

Praised

  • The story
  • The structure
  • The art

Criticised

  • The battles, as repetitive and detached from the story
  • Too much reading for the play
  • Vague lock hints
  • A prologue that splices battles between introductions
  • Thirteen routes open at once

Star Guardian

Cosmology and the squad

Riot's skin universe across four games. The first team in 2015–16 with its short film, a veteran team in 2017 with a co-op mode, the return of a destroyed first team in 2019, and a new generation in 2022 with a visual novel and a comic.

League of LegendsWild RiftLegends of RuneterraTeamfight Tactics
What it does

A cosmic power source

  • Grants fragments of itself to chosen people with strong will.
  • The power is a pact: a guardian fights until she dies or falls.
  • Fear, doubt and lost hope make her fall.

A darkness

  • It comes from outside and feeds on fear and doubt.
  • Its soldiers are void creatures.
  • Small pockets of it open around the city.

The fallen

  • A guardian who falls becomes the darkness's monster.
  • Another who broke over it hunts guardians to end the pact.
  • Puppeted former teammates hunt the survivor.
  • An infiltrator poses as a teacher and tempts the member who doubts.

Teams

  • Five, with a captain, a mentor and small companions.
  • By day they're students, with one civilian who knows the secret.
  • Rival teams distrust each other, then ally against something bigger.

Themes

  • Duty against freedom: nearly every antagonist is a guardian who rejected the oath.
  • The cost of power; found family; legacy and inherited trauma.
  • Doubt against hope; idols and pop music.

The look

  • Magical girl meets space opera.
  • A shared base outfit with one signature colour per member, star gems, sparkle.
  • The fallen keep their silhouettes in corrupted palettes and switch between forms mid-fight.
  • The art stays candy-bright over a tragic story.

Front Mission

The battle

Square's tactics series. Evolved is a shooter and Left Alive a stealth game; neither applies.

1 · 1995 · 1st: Remake 20222 · 1997 · remake 20233 · 1999 · remake 2025 4 · 20035 · 2005, Japan only2089 · mobile and DS Evolved · a shooterLeft Alive · a stealth game
What it does

Parts and targeting

  • Wanzers are built from parts: body, left arm, right arm, legs, each with its own HP.
  • Targeting is set by the weapon, and mostly random: melee leans to the body, machine guns and shotguns spread across parts, single shots hit one part.
  • Choosing a part needs a skill, and the 2023 remake's lack of it was its top complaint.

Weight against output

  • A wanzer's total weight can't pass its body's power output.
  • Backpacks add output or carry items, jump jets, sensors, repair kits or radios.
  • In the first game a lighter load gives extra movement.

Weapons in three bands

  • Melee: one hit.
  • Short range: multi-hit, can counter.
  • Long range: long reach, limited ammo, a minimum range; can't be countered and can't counter.
  • Damage types in 4 and 5: piercing, impact and fire, against an armour coat of one type.

Turns and links

  • Turns: phases in 1 to 3; Action Points from 2 on. Moving, attacking, countering and blocking spend one pool, so a unit that spends everything on its turn is defenceless on the enemy's.
  • Links: allies join an attack or take a hit for a partner. Setting them up in 4 was a chore; 5 simplified it.

Pilot skills and capture

  • Pilot skills trigger by chance (a second hit, firing the other hand, striking first, stunning, choosing a part).
  • In 3 they're learned at random by fighting with parts that carry them.
  • Capture: in 3 a pilot forced out of her machine can board any empty one, so enemy machines can be taken.

Between battles and the cutaway

  • Towns with shops, a setup screen, an arena with odds, simulators and, in 3, a pseudo-internet.
  • Presentation: every exchange cuts to a side-view duel that shows parts taking hits.
What players said

Loved

  • Customisation
  • Part damage that makes each fight specific
  • Links in 4 and 5
  • Mission variety

Frustrating

  • Slow pacing (unit counts, AP bookkeeping, passive AI that waits for scripts)
  • Hidden hit modifiers
  • Chance skills that decide fights
  • No part choice
  • Menu weight (no saved loadouts, part-by-part swaps for a dozen units)
  • Manual link setup
  • A fast mode in the 2025 remake that hid the part damage

Triangle Strategy

The look and the build-out
Square Enix and Artdink2022Unreal Engine 4Metacritic 83–85 Over a million sold
What it does

The look

  • HD-2D on larger, block-built maps than Octopath, with a camera that turns a full circle (which made every map edge a cost).
  • Sprites were tuned to read as pixel art at more realistic proportions.
  • Depth of field, bloom, vignette and chromatic aberration; reviewers found the image cleaner with depth of field off.
  • Rain leaves puddles, snow melts, wind lowers arrow accuracy.
  • Octopath's published recipe adds:
    • deep depth of field, so near and far stay sharp;
    • point lights that cast moving shadows;
    • a screen-space fade on anything in front of a character;
    • pulsing emissive masks.

Battle UX

  • A turn-order bar.
  • Tile colours: blue reachable and safe, purple reachable in enemy range, red lines from each enemy that could hit, red attack range, yellow area (the legend below).
  • A forecast of HP, damage and hit chance for the attack and the follow-up.
  • A simulation of out-of-range enemies.
  • A terrain card.
  • An autosave every turn and a retry.

Battle systems

  • Each unit's clock fills by Speed; a turn is a move and an action; waiting brings the next turn sooner.
  • TP: +1 a turn, a cap of 3 rising with promotions; skills cost it, basic attacks are free.
  • Follow-ups: an ally on the far side of the target attacks too.
  • Back attacks always crit; height adds damage and archer range.
  • Terrain: fire melts ice into puddles, lightning spreads across wet tiles and metal track; houses collapse; wind pushes.
  • Kudos from back attacks and follow-ups buy once-a-battle Quietuses.
  • No permadeath; retreat keeps XP.

Between battles

  • The Scales of Conviction: seven companions vote with tokens on key choices, after the hero tries to persuade each; some arguments only unlock with what exploration found.
  • Three hidden convictions move with dialogue and how you fight, and decide some recruits and the ending.
  • Exploration of the next battlefield.
  • The Encampment: a provisioner, a smithy that ranks up weapons, a trade post and mock battles that give XP even on a loss.

Ours: the forecast card and range colours, with a toggle that paints every enemy's reach (TC29).

A turn is a move and an action. D waits on turn 5, keeps part of its clock and acts again on turn 9 instead of at time 50 (the dashed line). Ours keeps the Speed timeline across the top (TC3). TP (+1 a turn, cap 3) becomes Charge (+1 a turn, cap 3, up to 5 with licences, TC20).

Praised

  • The map design
  • Follow-ups and scarce TP
  • How the votes tie into the story
  • The art

Criticised

  • An hour or more of cutscenes between battles, and a slow opening
  • Story chapters that are all cutscene
  • Convictions that feel predictable
  • Blur on Switch
  • Rooftop archers that trivialise maps

What the sibling games already settled

The pass over uma-quest, uma-farm and uma-platform set rules this game follows.

  • The cast is the Ranker's 100, one per outfit, in its ten batches of ten (uma-platform P131, P138, P139). The base outfit is the real Uma; sisters are Umas of their own; F means unused. Umas are keyed by outfit, never by character id alone, because a sister shares her base Uma's character id.
  • Roles come from an authored tag, not the running style. uma-quest moved its Ways to a hand-authored story class (its SC7, D50) and uma-farm its favourite line to the bible (RM6), after the owner said he was moving away from the class separation (P123). Running style only colours how she moves.
  • Stats: both games read the five stats per outfit. Every outfit's 5★ stats sum to 550, so only the spread differs.
  • Bosses come from the boards' casts in authoring order, the owner picks, the tools never rank, left-out units are never used, and tiers are never game rules (TS14). uma-quest fills encounter slots (Regulars by role, one Key, one Boss) from pools of board ids (its SP43).
  • Umas are adults and are never maimed. A damaged pose is weary with the costume intact.
  • Stories go through Uma Story's draft, review and publish (P141). A fourth book needs its own P-decision.
  • Art: the Combat Adult Character keys are side-on and face one way (uma-quest ships 12 a Uma, never mirrored, in its own private pack, D45). The chibi sets walk in four facings.
  • uma-stage today (0.3.0, rechecked in D4) (the concept §7, the build plan §3)
    It has
    • an orthographic camera pitched 35° with a fixed yaw, one texel per device pixel
    • a grid builder with heights 0–2
    • pure tile picking and A*
    • tilt-shift and bloom, which the battery saver turns off by default on phones
    • at most 8 live sprite sets
    • two finished scenes, uma-quest's field and uma-farm's band, so a grid is a game-owned scene from its parts
    It has no
    • range overlays
    • tactical camera
    • rotation
    • emissive glow
    • rain
    • bloom that can be tuned
  • Art since uma-platform P147: ChatGPT draws character art only, enemies included; Claude draws everything else in code, the HD-2D map kits included (scripts/kit-art).

Eight travelers, each with her own chapters

A starter, then home towns

Levels and path actions

  • Each chapter shows a recommended level on the map.
  • Each traveler brings a path action, a way to deal with people in towns.
