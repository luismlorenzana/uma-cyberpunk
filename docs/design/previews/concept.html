The short answer

Front Mission's rigs on Triangle Strategy's boards TC1TC2

A squad of four Umas, plus a guest, fights turn-based battles of about ten minutes on small HD-2D grid maps with height, on the owner's iPhone in landscape.

Combat rigs built from accessories TC17TC25

In place of Front Mission's robots: a Core, two arms and Legs that each take damage and break, plus a Back unit and a Visor. A broken arm drops its weapon, broken legs pin her down, a broken Core is a Rig Break: she's out of the battle, never hurt.

Speed decides who moves next TC3TC20TC21

On a timeline, as in Triangle Strategy. One small resource, Charge, pays for skills and also for counters and joining an ally's attack, so spending it all leaves an Uma open.

The setting is interplanetary D4TC6TC51TC53

A ship carries the squad between worlds. The first world is D3's neon city on an orbital ring. Each lead Uma has her own chapters, Octopath Traveler's way: her Chapter 1 plays on her home world, and finishing it brings her aboard. The world, its mystery and its story are written by the story session from the story brief, in 13 Sentinels' and Star Guardian's spirit.

The enemies are the eight franchises TC7TC37TC38TC26

On the platform's boards (Azur Lane, Genshin Impact, Wuthering Waves, Honkai: Star Rail, Zenless Zone Zero, Honkai Impact 3rd, Punishing: Gray Raven and NIKKE), as eight factions with original names, one new mechanic each. Every enemy character is a heroine, and a beaten heroine is freed, never killed; some join.

Between battles, the ship TC42TC49TC50TC34TC35

It works like Triangle Strategy's Encampment: a Hangar for rig parts, a Sim Pod, the Log of the mystery, the Bridge's star map, and the Call, where the crew votes on the big choices. It grows a room as each lead comes aboard.

No new character art blocks the start TC12

The map uses the Umas' existing four-facing chibi sets and the heroines' Ranker images as standees; attack cut-ins use the Umas' full-body renders until ChatGPT's Combat Adult keys arrive.

The order D4TC15TC54

The workspace, cloud and hello app (Phase 1); the combat, tested three ways: rules with tests, a sandbox on the phone and a headless simulator (Phase 2, which can start beside the cloud); then the whole app on test content: the ship, the star map, the crew, the Hangar, the mission flow and the story runner (Phase 3); then life aboard on test content (Phase 4, D20). The first worlds follow when the story session delivers them (Phase 5).

4.6Counters, Crossfire and Cover new D3TC21

Umas Enemies The target A wall

Counters are automatic

A defender counters when she has Charge, a Melee or Short weapon in range, and the attack comes from her front or side. Long and Tech attacks are never countered. An attack from behind gets past both her shield and her counter.

Crossfire

When an Uma attacks, up to two allies join with a free basic attack, each paying 1 Charge, if they have their link on and either stand on the far side of the target (Triangle Strategy's pincer) or have a Short or Long weapon in range with a line of fire (Front Mission's link). Enemies do it too.

Cover

An adjacent ally with a shield takes a hit for 1 Charge.

One link toggle

Each Uma has one link toggle; nothing to buy or set up.

Strain TC27

Mission → Mission → Mission → She sits the next one out, which clears it
  • Each mission adds 1 Strain to every Uma who fought; at 3 she sits the next one out.
  • The bench still earns half XP, and once a battle the squad can call a bench support from a resting Uma's role (a scan, a supply drop, a drone strike).
  • Overdrive skills add Strain too (WD3).
  • It replaces permadeath and pushes the cast to rotate, as 13 Sentinels' fatigue did.

4.12One exchange, worked through

  1. The timeline shows a Striker next. A Steel Muster trooper holds a cover tile ahead, facing her; a Gunner stands two tiles to her left with a line of fire.
  2. Move: tap the tile behind the trooper; her ghost and path appear (the route is out of the trooper's front arc). Tap again to move.
  3. Attack: tap the trooper. The forecast reads: blade, from behind, +50%, no counter, no shield; 92% hit; likely part Core; Crossfire: the Gunner joins (1 Charge, rifle, 78%, spread across parts); the trooper's Legs will break. Confirm.
  4. The cut-in plays (Quick): her blade, then the Gunner's rifle. The paper doll over the trooper shows the Core at a third and the Legs broken.
  5. Face: the arrows are preset toward the last target; she turns to face the next enemy's approach.
  6. The Gunner has spent her Charge, so on the trooper's turn she can't counter. That was the price.

Worlds → chapters → missions.

  • A chapter is a short arc led by one Uma: scenes and two to four missions, about three sessions.
  • Story missions move a chapter. Side jobs are repeatable missions on a world's map, for credits and parts, led by any Uma (D3's contracts).
  • Worlds ship as content waves (TC15). How many worlds, leads and chapters is the story session's (TC53).

Story tools TC32amended D4

13 Sentinels' devices stay, as features the story can use, no longer a fixed act shape:

  • a flash-forward: any mission can fix its squad and loadouts and play ahead of its chapter;
  • gates: a chapter can wait on another chapter, a mission or a File, and says so.

D3's Operations-and-Routes acts are dropped as a fixed form. TC33 amended D4 folds into TC51: D3's route leads are the lead Umas, and their genre lenses (WD19) are the story session's.

An example of one route through the first stretch: which leads, which worlds and which order are the story session's and the player's.

  • Her Chapter 1 plays on her home world and ends with her coming aboard, at the squad's level, never level 1.
  • The first Chapter 1 opens the game on the first world. Whether the player picks a starter, as Octopath does, is the story session's call; the game supports both. Octopath locks its starter in the party; this game doesn't, because Strain rotates the cast (TC27).
  • She's fixed in her own chapters; the player picks the other three and the guest.
  • Chapter 1s stay short (one or two battles) and never run back to back: the ship's route puts a later chapter or a crossed path between pickups.
  • Later chapters can play on any world. Each shows a danger level (a recommended squad level, in small steps) and names its keys on the world's map (TC34). Leads vary in shape: three to five chapters, some with a branch, and objectives other than "beat the boss".
  • The crew is in the cutscenes. Each scene has one to three short interjection slots, filled by whichever leads are in the squad, with a fallback line, and each chapter ends with a debrief aboard, replayable from the Log. Octopath's most cited flaw was leads absent from each other's stories.

Leads and the Log TC34amended D4

  • Leads come from scenes and battle side objectives (hack that terminal, save that civilian, free a heroine with a given Uma). A scene offers the Leads that apply as one-tap choices; used in another lead's chapter, one opens a branch or a File. No hunting for hidden spots, and no rewinds.

The Call TC35reworded D4

Triangle Strategy's vote, without hidden numbers.

  • At a big choice, the crew votes with tokens. Before the vote, one try to persuade each voter, with arguments that unlock through Intel scenes (a diorama with four to six hotspots, about three minutes) and the matching conviction.
  • Three convictions, CodeEdgeFree, move with choices and with how the squad fights. They're visible, and each voter shows her reasons.
  • A branch swaps the next one or two missions, then rejoins. Conviction thresholds decide which guests join.

A fixed camera

Orthographic, pitched 35°, one texel per device pixel, no rotation in v1 (rotation was Triangle Strategy's biggest cost, and the stage builds south faces only). A tile is about 43 × 24 pt on a 3× iPhone in landscape, so about 20 tiles fit across and 16 deep: a flat 14 × 14 board fits, and a board with height pans. Anything between the camera and the acting or target unit fades. Terrain rises toward the back of a map so heights hide little.

The look

Night by default (a dark blue-violet ambient, a cool key light, a grade per map); neon signs as glowing textures with their light painted on the ground; the stage's four live lights on the acting unit, the ability flash and two hero signs; bloom only on neon, flashes and holograms; a wide sharp band of tilt-shift in battle, a stronger one in story shots; no motion blur, chromatic aberration only as a pulse on a hit or a hack.

Rain

One scrolling streak layer, small splashes, and wet-tile kit variants with reflections painted in. Rain makes open tiles Wet (TC24).

The diorama edge

The board is a cut slab over far parallax layers (the world's skyline), with the lower layers on its front face.

It renders only when something moves

uma-farm's wake-and-sleep loop, which suits a turn-based game and the battery.

CY7 proves it on the phone

Before the app builds on it:

Combat testing, three ways TC54new D4

Before any content depends on the battle.

  1. The rules as a pure package, @cyber/battle: a deterministic reducer, uma-rpg's pattern. A battle is a frozen input (map, units, loadouts, seed) plus a list of actions; each action returns the new state and cues (what happened, for the scene to play back). Randomness is a seeded hash, never Math.random. The forecast calls the same pure functions the resolution uses and shows chances and ranges, never the seeded roll, so the card never lies and never spoils (TC29). Every rule has unit tests, and golden replays (an input, its actions and the exact cues) catch any change in behaviour. Rules carry a version, so an old save's battle still replays.
  2. A sandbox on the phone, uma-quest's test room grown up: a route that sets up any skirmish (map, squad, loadouts, enemies, seed), plays it with the full battle UI, and saves the setup as a scenario file to replay or share. The owner's play tests run here before any chapter exists.
  3. A headless simulator, uma-racer's pnpm sim and uma-rpg's balance plays: scenarios played AI against AI over many seeds, reporting win rates, battle length in turns and minutes, damage by band and type, parts broken, Charge spent, counters and Crossfires. The tuning numbers (bands, part HP, Capacity, damage) live in one table, so a balance pass is a table edit and a rerun.

The stack TC11

uma-rpg's: React, Vite, Tailwind, Zustand and Dexie on the web, NestJS on Cloud Run, Firebase Hosting and Auth, a PWA. It installs uma-data, uma-kit, uma-work, uma-server, uma-art and uma-stage, and copies uma-quest's wiring, save sync and PWA set-up (the build plan).

In the uma-rpg GCP project like uma-quest's and uma-farm's (uma-platform P88), every piece its own: site uma-cyberpunk, Cloud Run cyber-api, Firestore database cyber. CY2 creates it on the owner's go, with a free-tier cost table.
