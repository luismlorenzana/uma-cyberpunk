The short answer

Front Mission's rigs on Triangle Strategy's boards TC1TC2

A squad of four Umas, plus a guest, fights turn-based battles of about ten minutes on small HD-2D grid maps with height, on the owner's iPhone in landscape.

Combat rigs built from accessories TC17TC25

In place of Front Mission's robots: a Core, two arms and Legs that each take damage and break, plus a Back unit and a Visor. A broken arm drops its weapon, broken legs pin her down, a broken Core is a Rig Break: she's out of the battle, never hurt.

Speed decides who moves next TC3TC20TC21

On a timeline, as in Triangle Strategy. One small resource, Charge, pays for skills and also for counters and joining an ally's attack, so spending it all leaves an Uma open.

The setting TC6TC31

A neon megacity on an orbital ring that runs on light stolen from other worlds through its Gates. Heroines from eight other worlds come through to stop it, and the Ring's Authority points them at the squad. The truth comes out in five layers over five acts, 13 Sentinels' way, with Star Guardian's candy-bright neon over a tragic story (world page).

The enemies are the eight franchises TC7TC37TC38TC26

On the platform's boards (Azur Lane, Genshin Impact, Wuthering Waves, Honkai: Star Rail, Zenless Zone Zero, Honkai Impact 3rd, Punishing: Gray Raven and NIKKE), as eight factions with original names, one new mechanic each. Every enemy character is a heroine, and a beaten heroine is freed, never killed; some join.

Between battles, the Hideout TC42TC34TC35

It works like Triangle Strategy's Encampment: a Workshop for rig parts, a Sim Pod, the Archive of the mystery, and the Call, where the squad votes on the big choices.

No new character art blocks the start TC12

The map uses the Umas' existing four-facing chibi sets and the heroines' Ranker images as standees; attack cut-ins use the Umas' full-body renders until ChatGPT's Combat Adult keys arrive.

The order TC15

The workspace, cloud and hello app (Phase 1), a battle prototype with rigs on stand-ins (Phase 2), then Act I's opening (Phase 3).

Three pillars

  1. Tactics first. Every system serves the grid: position, facing, height, turn order, parts and loadout. Nothing auto-battles.
  2. Short and readable on a phone. Small maps, a squad of four, a forecast before every commit, Charge as a few big pips, and part damage as a paper doll, not a spreadsheet.
  3. Built from what exists. The stage, the kit, the save and sync, master data, the Ranker's boards and its images already work. This game adds the battle rules, the world and the story.

4.4The rig newTC17TC18

The Umas fight in a combat rig built from accessories, in place of Front Mission's robots. Four parts take damage and break; two are slots that can't be hit.

4.6Counters, Crossfire and Cover newTC21

Umas Enemies The target A wall

Counters are automatic

A defender counters when she has Charge, a Melee or Short weapon in range, and the attack comes from her front or side. Long and Tech attacks are never countered. An attack from behind gets past both her shield and her counter.

Crossfire

When an Uma attacks, up to two allies join with a free basic attack, each paying 1 Charge, if they have their link on and either stand on the far side of the target (Triangle Strategy's pincer) or have a Short or Long weapon in range with a line of fire (Front Mission's link). Enemies do it too.

Cover

An adjacent ally with a shield takes a hit for 1 Charge.

One link toggle

Each Uma has one link toggle; nothing to buy or set up.

Strain TC27

Mission → Mission → Mission → She sits the next one out, which clears it
  • Each mission adds 1 Strain to every Uma who fought; at 3 she sits the next one out.
  • The bench still earns half XP, and once a battle the squad can call a bench support from a resting Uma's role (a scan, a supply drop, a drone strike).
  • Overdrive skills add Strain too (WD3).
  • It replaces permadeath and pushes the cast to rotate, as 13 Sentinels' fatigue did.

4.12One exchange, worked through

  1. The timeline shows a Striker next. A Steel Muster trooper holds a cover tile ahead, facing her; a Gunner stands two tiles to her left with a line of fire.
  2. Move: tap the tile behind the trooper; her ghost and path appear (the route is out of the trooper's front arc). Tap again to move.
  3. Attack: tap the trooper. The forecast reads: blade, from behind, +50%, no counter, no shield; 92% hit; likely part Core; Crossfire: the Gunner joins (1 Charge, rifle, 78%, spread across parts); the trooper's Legs will break. Confirm.
  4. The cut-in plays (Quick): her blade, then the Gunner's rifle. The paper doll over the trooper shows the Core at a third and the Legs broken.
  5. Face: the arrows are preset toward the last target; she turns to face the next enemy's approach.
  6. The Gunner has spent her Charge, so on the trooper's turn she can't counter. That was the price.

The structure TC8amended D3

Acts → two tracks → chapters → missions. Story missions move the plot; contracts are repeatable missions for credits and parts, led by any Uma. The map starts as the Ring's layers and becomes a map of the Lanes in Act III.

The world bible TC31

It's the world page (world.md): the Tithe in five layers (WD1–WD24).

Two tracks that gate each other TC32

13 Sentinels' modes, made explicit. Operations are the act's spine: the Gate incursions, rising in difficulty, light on story. The act's first battle is a flash-forward to its last Operation, with a fixed squad (WD17). Routes are chapters led by one Uma. An Operation opens at a share of route progress; a route chapter can wait for an Operation; the act's last Operation needs every route finished.

The flash-forward, the act's first battle The share of route progress an Operation opens at A route chapter that waits for an Operation The last Operation needs every route

Which chapter sits where, and the shares, are an example.

Routes TC33

Six route leads an act, three or four open at a time, three chapters each. A route chapter fixes its lead; the player picks the other three and the guest. Each route has its own genre lens and ends on the shared beat: her signature unlocks and she joins the last Operation (WD19).

Leads and the Archive TC34

  • Leads come from scenes and battle side objectives (hack that terminal, save that civilian, free a heroine with a given Uma). A scene offers the Leads that apply as one-tap choices; used in another Uma's route, one opens a branch or a File. No hunting for hidden spots, and no rewinds.
  • The Archive in the Hideout: Files on people, districts, factions and Gate-worlds, with redacted lines that fill in; a Timeline of every scene, by place and date, replayable; Anomalies, the contradictions the player has seen, open until explained (WD18).
  • Every lock names its key on the route board: which chapter, Operation or File opens it.
  • Freed heroines open their File and a short log from their side (WD12).

The Call TC35

Triangle Strategy's vote, without hidden numbers.

  • At a big choice, the squad votes with tokens. Before the vote, one try to persuade each voter, with arguments that unlock through Intel scenes (a district diorama with four to six hotspots, about three minutes) and the matching conviction.
  • Three convictions, CodeEdgeFree, move with choices and with how the squad fights. They're visible, and each voter shows her reasons.
  • A branch swaps the next one or two missions, then rejoins. The act's last vote shapes its ending, and conviction thresholds decide which guests join.

Levels1–50 per Uma; the bench earns half (TC27).

7How it's built TC10TC11TC14TC48

The battle view TC10amended D3: an HD-2D diorama on @luismlorenzana/uma-stage (0.2.0, uma-platform P142), in Triangle Strategy's look.

A game-owned battle scene

Built from the stage's parts, as uma-quest's fights already are: its grid builder, tile picking, A*, sprite actor, camera maths, tilt-shift and bloom. The range overlays, the timeline and the cut-ins are this game's own (DOM over the canvas for lasting UI).

A fixed camera

Orthographic, pitched 35°, one texel per device pixel, no rotation in v1 (rotation was Triangle Strategy's biggest cost, and the stage builds south faces only). Anything between the camera and the acting or target unit fades. Terrain rises toward the back of a map so heights hide little.

The look

Night by default (a dark blue-violet ambient, a cool key light, a grade per map); neon signs as glowing textures with their light painted on the ground; the stage's four live lights on the acting unit, the ability flash and two hero signs; bloom only on neon, flashes and holograms; a wide sharp band of tilt-shift in battle, a stronger one in story shots; no motion blur, chromatic aberration only as a pulse on a hit or a hack.

Rain

One scrolling streak layer, small splashes, and wet-tile kit variants with reflections painted in. Rain makes open tiles Wet (TC24).

The diorama edge

The board is a cut slab over far parallax layers (the city, the Ring's arc), with the Underlevels' layers on its front face.

CY4 is a spike

It proves it on the phone before anything builds on it:

  • a 14 × 14 grid with heights at 35° in landscape;
  • tile picking at about 24 pt tile depth;
  • tilt-shift and bloom with battery saver off;
  • about thirteen figures live (four Umas' chibi sets, and standees for the guest and eight enemies);
  • how much heights hide.

If it fails, the fallback is a Phaser 4 isometric board, the platform's baseline for 2D scenes.

The stage requests TC48

CY4 files with uma-platform only what the spike shows is needed, as requests, never a fork:

  1. A grid stage: figures, picking and grade over a room-format grid, with a free two-axis pan.
  2. Heights 0–3 in the format, and a jump step.
  3. Tile overlays and highlights (ranges, a cursor), if drawing them game-side costs too much.
  4. A tactical camera: a steeper pitch or a zoom, with the pixel rule relaxed.
  5. Glowing kit frames and tunable bloom and tilt-shift.
  6. Rain particles.
  7. A lighter tier of effects that stays on under battery saver, which is on by default on phones.

Rotation in 90° steps stays deferred (TC16). Attacks in more facings aren't needed: the cut-in carries the attack.

The stack TC11

uma-rpg's: React, Vite, Tailwind, Zustand and Dexie on the web, NestJS on Cloud Run, Firebase Hosting and Auth, a PWA. It installs uma-data, uma-kit, uma-work, uma-server, uma-art and uma-stage.

The cloud TC11

In the uma-rpg GCP project like uma-quest's and uma-farm's (uma-platform P88), every piece its own: site uma-cyberpunk, Cloud Run cyber-api, Firestore database cyber. CY2 creates it on the owner's go, with a free-tier cost table.

It stands alone TC14

Its own save, rules and economy, and no parcels from uma-farm at the start. Parcels can come later the way uma-quest takes them (uma-platform P89), on the owner's word.
